## Audience and jobs to be done

### Primary audience

Evidence-supported audience: eligible residents of the five stated states who want compassionate online mental-health care and need a clear next step for evaluation, medication management, therapy, or coordinated care.

### Likely decision needs

These are medium-confidence marketing inferences that still need patient-language validation:

- Determine whether appropriate care is available in the patient's state.
- Understand the difference between evaluation, medication management, and therapy.
- Confirm provider credentials, focus areas, ages served, insurance or self-pay pathways, and availability.
- Know what happens during a first visit and what the next step is.
- Feel that the practice is credible and personal rather than a directory or anonymous platform.

### Anti-persona and exclusions

- Anyone needing emergency or crisis care; crisis resources must remain separate and unmistakable.
- Anyone outside verified state, provider, service, age, or legal eligibility.
- Anyone expecting guaranteed outcomes, guaranteed prescriptions, or instant treatment.

## Positioning and differentiation

Working position for review:

> Compassionate online mental-health care that connects psychiatric evaluation, medication management, and therapy across five licensed states.

Working emotional promise: `Comfort. Peace of mind. Hope.`

Evidence-supported differentiation hypothesis:

- More personal than a national provider directory.
- Broader and more coordinated than a solo prescriber or single-service provider.
- Clearer about state eligibility, provider fit, and the care journey than disconnected service pages.

These are strategic comparison statements, not validated customer claims. Do not use superlatives such as "best," unverified speed claims, or promises about access, insurance, prescriptions, or outcomes.

## Message hierarchy

The first screen and every high-intent landing page should answer, in order:

1. What care is offered?
2. Where is it available?
3. Why should the patient trust Hope?
4. Is the service appropriate for the patient's need?
5. What is the lowest-pressure accurate next action?

Preferred calls to action:

- Check availability.
- Find a provider.
- Schedule a first appointment.
- See services available in your state.

Avoid outcome promises, "available everywhere," "start treatment instantly," or language that implies an office where none exists.

## Voice and content standard

Voice: calm, warm, credible, human, clinically restrained, and plain-language.

Do:

- Pair emotional reassurance with concrete service and eligibility clarity.
- Explain care options and first steps without jargon.
- Name the clinical author or reviewer, credentials, review date, and sources on priority medical content.
- Use bounded phrasing such as "may be available when clinically appropriate and legally permitted."
- Keep patient autonomy and a low-pressure next step visible.

Do not:

- Diagnose, prescribe, or provide individualized medical advice in marketing copy.
- Guarantee outcomes, access speed, insurance coverage, prescriptions, or controlled medications.
- Use fake local-office signals or thin state pages with only the state name swapped.
- Publish controlled-substance or substance-use claims without current federal, state, and clinical review.

No verified verbatim patient-language corpus is available. Until one is collected with appropriate consent and privacy controls, treat all audience language above as a working messaging system rather than customer quotation evidence.

## Visual and video standard

- Preserve the supplied Hope logo exactly; never regenerate, redraw, morph, or place it in false 3D perspective.
- Use calm pacing, breathing room, eased motion, purposeful pauses, and warm human framing.
- Favor brand-compatible blue, sage, cream, and solid mid-tone wardrobe choices where the supplied asset system supports them.
- Illustrations may support emotion and education, but must not depict diagnosis, guaranteed transformation, or clinical outcomes.
- Therapist video must protect privacy: no patient information, visible records, personal identifiers, or unapproved patient stories.
- Provider footage requires likeness or content approval and a named clinical reviewer before public use.

## Growth and conversion system

Recommended path:

`state or service intent -> accurate landing page -> provider and eligibility proof -> availability or booking action -> qualified inquiry -> operational outcome`

Measurement should prioritize qualified appointment actions by state and service. Track visibility and engagement as leading indicators, not as the final result.

Required event contract from the 2026-07-15 analysis:

- `appointment_click`
- `booking_start`
- `booking_complete`
- `contact_form_start`
- `contact_form_submit`
- `phone_click`
- `provider_view`
- `state_page_engagement`
- `video_50`
- `video_complete`

Capture 28-day and 90-day baselines before major releases. Segment by state, service page, device, source or medium, and new versus returning user. Report lead and conversion-event counts as whole integers and keep paid, organic, referral, and direct results separate.
