Executive diagnosis

Hope has the right ingredients. The website does not yet assemble them into one obvious reason to choose the practice.

The strongest growth opportunity is not “more content” in the abstract. It is alignment: search language, trust proof, state availability, provider authority, booking flow, and the new visual system should all tell the same story within the first screen and continue that story through conversion.

The central mismatch

The homepage title is currently “Outpatient Mental Health Clinic” and its H1 is the warm brand line “Helping you find comfort, peace of mind, and hope!” The emotional language is strong, but it does not immediately answer the highest-intent questions: What care is offered? Is it online? Is it available in my state? Can I book?

Keep the warmth as the emotional promise. Move service, telehealth, and state clarity into the headline and trust strip.

Recommended positioning

Primary: Compassionate online mental health care that connects psychiatric evaluation, medication management, and therapy across five licensed states.

Proof: A real clinical team, condition-specific care, convenient secure video visits, clear provider credentials, and a direct path to availability.

Difference: More personal than a national directory, broader than a solo prescriber, and more coordinated than a collection of disconnected service pages.

Critical compliance correction Hope’s website describes the practice as telehealth-only and says it does not offer in-person sessions. Google’s current Business Profile rules require in-person customer contact and list online-only businesses as ineligible. Do not create additional state profiles until Hope’s real-world operating model and eligibility have been documented. This supersedes the earlier automatic “add profiles” recommendation.

Conversion architecture

Recommended homepage and landing-page system

The homepage should answer five questions in order: What do you do? Where can I receive care? Can I trust you? Is this right for me? What should I do next?

Preferred first-screen copy

H1: Online Psychiatry, Therapy & Medication Management in RI, MA, NY, CO & AZ

Subhead: Personalized mental health care rooted in compassion, trust, and clinical expertise — delivered through secure video visits.

Clinical review required before publishing “psychiatry” or any specific service/state combination. If the provider mix makes that wording too broad, use: “Online Mental Health Care, Psychiatric Evaluations & Medication Management…”

Page hierarchy

1. Hero — descriptive H1, human promise, primary “Check availability” CTA, secondary “Meet our providers” CTA, exact logo and calm visual.
2. Trust strip — telehealth-only, states served, secure video care, verified insurance/payment language, direct phone.
3. Choose a care path — evaluation, medication management, therapy, and substance-use care with plain-language “who this is for.”
4. How it works — check state/insurance, select or match with provider, attend secure video visit.
5. Conditions and needs — ADHD, anxiety, depression, bipolar disorder, trauma, and other verified areas.
6. Meet the team — credentials, licensed states, focus, ages served, availability, and one human introduction video.
7. Cost and insurance clarity — verified plan language, self-pay pathway, and “coverage varies by plan/provider” guardrail.
8. Frequently asked questions — first visit, prescriptions, telehealth setup, emergencies, state eligibility, refills, and privacy.
9. Final CTA — “Find the right next step” with phone and online option; crisis resources remain separate and unmistakable.

Design rules

Calm pace

Use breathing room, eased motion, and purposeful pauses. Avoid frantic zooms or trend effects that undermine trust.

Exact brand

Use the supplied logo unchanged. Never regenerate, morph, redraw, or place it in 3D perspective.

Clinical restraint

Illustrations support the message; they do not depict diagnoses, outcomes, or guaranteed transformation.

Therapist-shot video

Dress-rehearsal and capture standard

The upcoming three rehearsal sessions should produce repeatable proof, not just camera tests. Each participant should leave with a validated setup, one approved introduction, and a clear next recording assignment.

Rehearsal checklist

AreaPass standardCommon failure to catch
FramingVertical 9:16; eye line upper third; chest-up; clean headroom; lens at eye level.Looking down at a laptop, excessive space, cropped hair, unstable tripod.
LightFace is brighter than background; soft window or key light 30–45° off camera.Backlighting, overhead shadows, mixed color temperatures.
SoundQuiet room, phone 2–4 feet away or approved mic, test playback on headphones.HVAC hum, echo, notifications, low volume.
BackgroundSimple, warm, uncluttered, no patient information, no distracting artwork.Visible documents, doors opening, personal/clinical identifiers.
WardrobeSolid mid-tone colors; brand-friendly navy, blue, sage, cream; natural styling.Fine patterns, pure white against a bright wall, noisy jewelry, logos.
DeliveryOne thought per take; conversational pace; 2-second handles before/after.Reading a paragraph, jargon, rushing, clinical promises.

Capture specification

  • Primary: vertical 4K/30 fps if the phone supports it; 1080p/30 minimum.
  • Clean lens, airplane/do-not-disturb mode, exposure/focus locked on the face.
  • Record three takes: natural, shorter, and one with a warmer opening.
  • If time allows, record a second horizontal 4K take for website use.
  • File name: YYYYMMDD_provider_topic_take01_vertical.mov.

Three script templates

10 seconds

Warm introduction

“Hi, I’m [name]. I help [audience] work through [need] with a practical, compassionate approach.”

20 seconds

What to expect

“Your first visit is a chance for us to understand what has been happening, what matters to you, and what kind of support may fit. We’ll talk through next steps together.”

30 seconds

One useful insight

“A common misconception about [topic] is [myth]. What we actually look for is [bounded explanation]. If this sounds familiar, an evaluation can help clarify the next step.”

Safety and consentNo patient information, patient stories, individualized medical advice, guaranteed outcomes, or unreviewed prescription claims. Obtain written provider likeness/content approval before public use. Clinical content receives a named reviewer before publishing.
