# How to Prepare for a Commercial Cleaning Walkthrough

**Before a commercial cleaning walkthrough, gather the building’s approximate cleanable area, operating schedule, room list, traffic patterns, surface types, access rules, current pain points and desired service frequency.** Better input produces a clearer scope and fewer surprises after service begins.

## Bring the right building information

Useful items include a floor plan, room schedule, approximate square footage by use, number of restrooms and fixtures, floor-material list and normal occupancy. Note seasonal peaks, events, deliveries and shifts that change traffic.

If exact measurements are unavailable, do not guess precision. Mark areas for field verification and distinguish total building area from the space the cleaning company will actually service.

## Identify access and operating constraints

Explain keys, badges, alarms, secure rooms, loading access, parking, elevator rules and lock-up expectations. Name an after-hours contact and identify spaces cleaners may enter only with an escort. Discuss where supplies and equipment may be stored without blocking an accessible route or egress.

Review AMI’s [commercial cleaning services](https://www.ami-cleaning.com/services/) before the visit so you can separate routine janitorial work from periodic floor care, windows, construction cleanup or daytime service.

## Show the building at its problem points

Walk the route occupants actually use. Point out entrance soil, high-volume restrooms, food areas, waste bottlenecks, floor wear, fingerprints, recurring spills and areas with complaints. A representative day is more informative than a freshly staged room.

Bring product restrictions, manufacturer care instructions, facility policies and known sensitivities. When disinfection is required, specify the approved products and procedure. The [CDC recommends following product labels](https://www.cdc.gov/hygiene/about/when-and-how-to-clean-and-disinfect-a-facility.html), including contact time, and cleaning visibly dirty surfaces before disinfecting.

## Define the quality standard

Discuss what the facility will inspect, how concerns are reported and how quickly urgent issues should be acknowledged. Ask for responsibilities by room, task and frequency. Clarify what is excluded, what counts as periodic work and how unexpected requests are approved.

## Questions to ask the cleaning company

- Who supervises the account and performs inspections?
- How are cleaners trained for this site?
- How are substitutions, absences and building-access issues handled?
- Which products and equipment are proposed?
- How will service changes be documented?
- What information is needed before pricing is final?

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does a commercial cleaning walkthrough take?

It depends on size and complexity. A small office may take less than an hour; a multi-building or industrial site may require more time and follow-up measurements.

### Should the current cleaning checklist be shared?

Yes, if available. Also explain where it succeeds or fails so the new scope does not simply repeat old assumptions.

### Can a price be finalized during the walkthrough?

Sometimes, but complex sites may require measurements, staffing review or product and access confirmation before a responsible proposal is complete.
