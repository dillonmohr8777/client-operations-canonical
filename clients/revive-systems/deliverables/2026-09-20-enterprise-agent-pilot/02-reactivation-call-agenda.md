## Discovery questionnaire

### A. Reactivation: audience and permission

1. What does “reactivation” need to accomplish first: a human conversation,
   an application, a referral/review, or another measurable outcome?
2. Which group is in scope for the first small test: past Revive clients,
   former gym members, referrals, or another named cohort?
3. What system or client-owned source contains the names? Who owns that source,
   and what is the last verified date range?
4. For each proposed channel (email, SMS, phone, Facebook/Instagram DM, or
   another channel), what documented permission and opt-out/suppression status
   exists? Who can verify it?
5. Are any people excluded because they opted out, asked not to be contacted,
   are not appropriate for the offer, or have an unresolved service issue?
6. What minimum fields are actually needed to plan the work? Can the first
   review use counts and redacted identifiers rather than a raw export?
7. Who responds to a reply, within what target time, and where is the response
   recorded? What happens when the person asks for medical, nutrition, or
   billing advice outside the coaching scope?
8. What is the approved first CTA? Confirm the exact URL or human handoff only
   after the client verifies it; do not substitute a guessed URL.
9. What evidence would count as success: qualified conversations, approved
   application starts, completed applications, referrals/reviews, or another
   defined outcome? What is the review window?
10. What message, incentive, testimonial, price, capacity statement, or offer
    inclusion is explicitly approved? What must not be said?

### B. Reactivation: message and safety

1. What would make the outreach useful and respectful to someone who has not
   engaged recently?
2. Should the message lead with a check-in, practical education, an invitation
   to talk, or an approved entry path? Why is that appropriate for this cohort?
3. What tone should be used: direct, encouraging, practical, credible, and
   non-judgmental? Which words or claims should be avoided?
4. How will a “not now,” “stop,” or no-response outcome be recorded and
   suppressed? Who owns that record?
5. What is the smallest reversible pilot that can answer the question without
   mass contact, paid promotion, public posting, or automated enrollment?

### C. Facebook Business profile diagnosis

1. What exact asset is meant by “Facebook Business profile”: public Page,
   personal profile, Meta Business portfolio, Business Suite, ad account, or
   connected Instagram asset? Capture its public URL or asset ID only if the
   client can provide it safely.
2. What is the observed problem in plain language: cannot find it, wrong owner,
   missing admin, duplicate asset, disabled/restricted, wrong business details,
   broken connection, or an unknown error?
3. When was the problem first noticed, by whom, and what changed immediately
   before it? Record exact error text and date from a client-provided screenshot
   if available; do not ask for a password or MFA code.
4. Who is the current business owner and who, if anyone, has admin/full-control
   access? Is the ownership relationship documented in the client’s records?
5. Is there more than one Page, portfolio, ad account, or Instagram connection?
   Which one is the intended canonical asset, and which duplicates must remain
   untouched pending review?
6. What is the desired end state: recover an existing asset, correct roles,
   reconcile duplicates, review a restriction, or document that the asset is
   not yet ready?
7. Can diagnosis be completed from a client screenshare or redacted screenshots?
   If authorized access is later needed, who will perform the human login and
   MFA, and what exact read-only check is requested?
8. What public profile facts are verified and approved (name, location, hours,
   services, contact path, imagery)? Do not infer or publish corrections from
   memory.
9. Does the Facebook issue affect the reactivation plan, or should the two
   tracks remain separate until ownership/access is clear?
10. What evidence would close the diagnosis: asset identity, owner, role state,
    error state, desired state, and a client-approved next action?

## Compliant reactivation boundaries

- Use only a client-provided or client-approved source list. Do not scrape
  members, buy lists, infer identities, enrich personal data, or upload a list
  to Meta/Facebook for matching without a separate exact approval and the
  client’s documented permission basis.
- Verify channel permission and suppression status before any contact. Honor
  opt-outs immediately, keep the cohort narrow, and do not use a historical
  membership alone as proof of current permission.
- Keep health, diagnosis, medication, body measurements, and other sensitive
  details out of public comments, social posts, shared drafts, and casual DMs.
  Ask people to use the approved private application or conversation route for
  details, after that route is verified.
- Do not make guarantees, medical-treatment claims, unsupported testimonials,
  invented results, prices, capacity claims, or automatic-enrollment promises.
  The verified voice is practical, credible, encouraging, and non-judgmental.
- Do not create or edit a Facebook/Meta asset, change roles, request access,
  accept an invitation, submit a form, publish, schedule, run ads, change
  tracking, or edit a HighLevel workflow from this agenda.
- Store only a redacted decision log and safe source locators. Keep raw client
  lists, screenshots with personal data, credentials, cookies, and MFA material
  out of this project artifact.
