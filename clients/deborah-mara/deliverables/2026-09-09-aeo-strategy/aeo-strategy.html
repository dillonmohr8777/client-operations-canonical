The shift

Buyers stopped clicking. They started asking.

"Who's a good realtor in Monmouth County?" used to return ten blue links. Increasingly it returns one paragraph and three names. If Deborah is not one of the three, the other seven links no longer matter.

Answer engines do not rank pages the way search engines do. They assemble an answer from sources they can read, verify and corroborate. That changes what wins. Volume of content matters less. Being unambiguously machine-readable matters enormously.

Why this suits Deborah specifically

Deborah is not going to write blogs, and this plan does not ask her to. Almost everything that drives AI visibility is configuration, not composition — structured data, crawler permissions, entity consistency and a handful of evergreen pages built once. It is a fixed amount of work, not a treadmill.

Pillar one

Let the AI crawlers in — explicitly.

The site's robots.txt today contains a bare Disallow: under a single wildcard rule. That is permissive by accident rather than by decision, and it names none of the agents that actually matter now. Make the invitation explicit, and audit it later.

The three OpenAI agents do different jobs and all three are worth allowing: GPTBot gathers training data, OAI-SearchBot builds the search index that ChatGPT cites, and ChatGPT-User fetches a page live when a user's question needs it. Blocking the second two removes her from citations.

Do this before launch, not after

The staging site is currently index, follow with a live sitemap and a page title of "Home - WordPress Blog". Crawlers may already be indexing an unfinished site under a default name. Either noindex it until launch, or finish the basics now. Both are settings changes, not development.

Pillar two

Publish an llms.txt.

An emerging convention: a single plain-language file at /llms.txt that tells a language model what this site is and what matters on it, without making it infer from navigation. It costs one file. Adoption is still uneven, so treat it as cheap upside rather than a guarantee.

The sameAs array is doing the heavy lifting

Answer engines resolve identity by corroboration. When the same name, phone and brokerage appear consistently across her site, Google Business Profile, homes.com, RateMyAgent and her social profiles, she becomes a confidently-known entity rather than a string of text. That confidence is what earns a citation.

Measurement

How we will know it is working.

AI visibility is harder to measure than rankings, so measure it three ways and do not pretend any one of them is complete.

  1. Referral traffic in GA4. Segment referrers for chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai, claude.ai and Gemini. Small numbers, high intent.
  2. Crawler hits in server logs. Confirm GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot and ClaudeBot are actually fetching pages. This is the earliest signal that the configuration took.
  3. Prompt testing, monthly. Ask the engines directly — "best realtor in Monmouth County NJ", "who should I talk to about selling a 55+ home in central New Jersey" — and record whether she is named. Manual, imperfect, and the only true measure of the outcome we are buying.
Set expectations honestly

Answer engines re-crawl and re-index on their own schedules. Structured data and crawler permissions can take weeks to surface, and no agency can guarantee a citation. What we control is making her the most machine-legible, best-corroborated agent in her three counties — which is what moves the odds.

One constraint that governs every page and ad

US real estate advertising falls under the Fair Housing Act. Copy must never suggest a preference or steer by race, colour, religion, sex, familial status, national origin or disability. The 55+ material is lawful under the Housing for Older Persons exception, but only when it refers to genuinely age-restricted communities. Her broker at RE/MAX Homeland REALTORS should review the pages before they go live.
