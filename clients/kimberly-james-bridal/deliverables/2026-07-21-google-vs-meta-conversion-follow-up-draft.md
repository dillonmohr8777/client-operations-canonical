# KJB Google versus Meta conversion follow-up

Date: 2026-07-21
Status: verified local Slack draft; unsent

Checked it end to end. The appointment request came through Google, not Meta.

Google's active Performance Max campaign, `Campaign #1`, sent traffic through `Asset Group 1` to:

https://www.kimberlyjamesbridal.com/bridal-appointment-request

For July 7 through July 20, Google shows $277.42 spent, 595 clicks, and one website lead submission, which is a platform CPL of $277.42. The first-party appointment-request sheet contains a July 20 submission from that page with a Google referrer and Google click identifiers.

Meta's active traffic campaign generated 224 landing-page views at $0.43 per view during the same period, but no leads. That means Meta does not have a measurable CPL for this window.

One important caveat: the form record proves a Google-sourced appointment request. It does not independently prove that the boutique confirmed the requested appointment. Also, Performance Max assembles creative dynamically, so the defensible ad identification is the campaign and asset group, not one single fixed image or headline.

There is also an attribution-ID discrepancy to investigate: the captured landing-page visit carried campaign ID `23695071022`, while the visible active Google Ads campaign is `23690389385`. That does not change the Google-versus-Meta conclusion, but it should be resolved before using campaign-ID-level reporting as the source of truth.
