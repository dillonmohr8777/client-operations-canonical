## Phase Two growth model

| Pillar | Role | Example work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Programs and offers | Give people a clear reason to engage | Workshops, cohorts, clinics, incubators, and existing service lines |
| Demand generation | Put the right opportunity in front of the right audience | Ad Grants, social, local outreach, partners, email, SMS, webinars, and selective paid support |
| Conversion and nurture | Turn awareness into participation | Landing or registration paths, scheduling, routing, reminders, follow-up, and referral tracking |
| Authority and discoverability | Build compounding trust and reach | Video, workshop repurposing, SEO, AEO, bilingual resources, and expert positioning |

## Campaign operating loop

`offer -> intended audience -> promotion -> landing or registration path -> response routing and nurture -> participant or client outcome -> reporting`

For each selected priority:

1. Confirm the offer, eligibility, audience, owner, and desired outcome.
2. Build the landing, registration, consultation, or referral path.
3. Select the smallest channel mix that fits the offer and audience.
4. Route every response and define follow-up ownership.
5. Repurpose the program, workshop, or expert input into useful content.
6. Measure participation quality and downstream outcomes.

## Voice standard

Voice: warm, welcoming, community-rooted, practical, respectful, access-oriented, and clear.

Do:

- Lead with participant value, eligibility, and the real community outcome.
- Use plain language and explain the next step.
- Present opportunities as choices to prioritize together.
- Keep the tone nonprofit and public-service oriented rather than commercial.
- Describe technology and channels in terms of what they enable for people and programs.

Do not:

- Sound salesy, extractive, or startup-like.
- Treat community members as clicks or raw lead volume.
- Promise program launch, funding, eligibility, enrollment, or results without approval.
- State that Google Ad Grant funding is secured.
- invent metrics, budgets, testimonials, program capacity, or timelines beyond the specified 90-day pilot.
- Reproduce internal chat, personal contact details, or team commentary in client-facing materials.

No approved Phase Two verbatim participant-language corpus was found. The April tax-prep language is too narrow to serve as the voice of the whole Economic Development portfolio.

## Outcomes and measurement

Primary outcomes:

- Qualified participants or clients by program.
- Workshop registrations and attendance.
- Partner and referral activity.
- Consultation or appointment bookings.
- Enrollment, application, or service completion where NKCDC can share it.

Supporting growth indicators:

- Qualified inquiries and response rates.
- Email and SMS list growth and engagement.
- Landing-page conversion rates.
- Outreach replies and partnership conversations.
- Search visibility, AI citations, video views, and social engagement.

Supporting indicators cannot substitute for program outcomes. Keep lead and conversion-event counts as whole integers and label source, period, and definition.

## 90-day pilot structure

### Month 1: Prioritize and build

- Choose two or three priorities.
- Confirm audiences, offers, owners, and conversion paths.
- Build required pages, tracking, and follow-up.
- Prepare the first assets and approved outreach inputs.

### Month 2: Launch and activate

- Launch the selected campaigns.
- Begin Ad Grants work only if eligible and approved.
- Activate the chosen outreach, social, email, SMS, and partner channels.
- Produce the first workshop, webinar, or expert-led content.

### Month 3: Optimize and scale

- Compare lead quality, registrations, attendance, and downstream outcomes.
- Improve the offer, audience, creative, and follow-up based on evidence.
- Expand the strongest program or campaign.
- Agree on the next quarterly roadmap.
