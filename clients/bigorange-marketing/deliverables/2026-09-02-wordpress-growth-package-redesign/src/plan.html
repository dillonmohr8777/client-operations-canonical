Every indexable page

The page-by-page optimization contract

Every public page needs one clear job and one owner. SEO gets you found, AEO gets your answer used, GEO gets you cited as a real source, and the conversion work makes the next step honest and easy to take.

01

Intent and query

One primary query family, search intent, audience, funnel job and non-cannibalizing parent relationship

02

Visible structure

One H1; direct answer near the top; descriptive H2s; scannable definitions, steps, comparisons or tables where useful

03

Trust and proof

Named author or reviewer; current dates; approved project evidence; precise attribution; no unsupported guarantees

04

SEO metadata

Unique title and description; canonical; index/follow decision; social title/image; sitemap eligibility

05

AEO

Answer-first passages; quotable definitions; concise FAQs written for readers; entity names and relationships stated plainly

06

GEO

Original information gain; stable citations; local/service context; consistent organization, people, services and locations

07

Schema

Only types supported by visible content; Article/BlogPosting, BreadcrumbList, Organization, Service or LocalBusiness as appropriate

08

Media and access

Descriptive filename, dimensions, compression, lazy loading below the fold, meaningful alt text, keyboard/focus and contrast review

09

Links and action

One parent link, relevant sibling links, contextual CTA, no broken redirects, no orphan page

10

Measurement

Search Console query/page baseline, analytics engagement, form or booking event, CRM source and sales acceptance

Cannibalization rule

The builder hub owns the broad commercial intent; each blog owns one narrower question. If Search Console later shows two URLs fighting over the same query, strengthen whichever one should own it, merge the redundant passages, update anchor text, and only consolidate once the evidence backs it up.

07
Stage, verify, then publish

WordPress backend release checklist

The five posts go into WordPress as private drafts. Nothing goes public until the release owner has confirmed both the visible content and every technical field.

  1. Create a draft post with the proposed slug; never reuse an existing public slug without a redirect and canonical decision.
  2. Select the correct author, category and review owner. Remove irrelevant default tags.
  3. Add the approved featured image, meaningful alt text, caption or credit when required, and social sharing image.
  4. Set title, meta description, canonical and robots. Confirm index, follow only at release time.
  5. Keep one visible H1 supplied by the theme or editor, not both. Verify heading order in the rendered preview.
  6. Insert the visible article, not metadata notes. Validate every internal and external link.
  7. Add BlogPosting and Breadcrumb structured data only when fields match visible content. Keep FAQ schema as semantic candidate only, without rich-result claims.
  8. Preview desktop and mobile. Check keyboard focus, forms, contrast, overflow, console errors and page speed.
  9. Run schema validation, sitemap discovery and Search Console URL inspection after publication. Sitemap inclusion is not proof of indexing.
  10. Record post ID, final URL, reviewer, approval date, publish date and first 30-day measurement checkpoint.
08
Reporting

Measurement from discovery to revenue truth

Ranking and traffic are leading evidence, not the finish line. The reporting needs to keep the whole chain intact: query, landing page, meaningful action, qualified inquiry, CRM opportunity, and the sales outcome.

1

Discovery

Search Console impressions, queries, position and page

Is the intended page being retrieved for the intended query family?

2

Engagement

Analytics engaged sessions, scroll and next-page behavior

Did the answer help the visitor continue?

3

Action

Verified form, booking, phone or email event

Did the page create a real hand raise?

4

Qualification

CRM source, service, market, timing and fit

Was the inquiry accepted by sales?

5

Outcome

Opportunity and closed business

What content assisted a commercially meaningful result?

6

Learning

Sales notes, lost reasons and content requests

What should be improved, expanded or retired?

Recommended cadence

  1. Weekly: indexing, broken links, form delivery and anomaly checks.
  2. Monthly: query-to-page ownership, content decay, qualified-inquiry review and editorial backlog.
  3. Quarterly: technical crawl, schema coverage, internal-link graph, page consolidation and conversion-path review.
