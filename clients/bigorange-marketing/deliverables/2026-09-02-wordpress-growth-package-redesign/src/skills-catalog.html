Purpose

This is the one-page-per-skill guide to the tools built around BigOrange's brand, decks, clients and industries. Each skill is a folder that any Claude Code or Codex session can load. Together they let the team produce on-brand decks and documents from a short brief, keep a roster of BigOrange's own clients current, and pitch each of the four industries with sourced, dated material. Nothing here sends, publishes or changes WordPress on its own.

The deck engine

A brief in, a validated BigOrange deck out. Recipes hold the slides BigOrange always says the same way (the method, the measurement chain, the release gates); the brief supplies what changes per client or industry.

RecipeAudienceFilled fromSlides
Industry pitchA prospect in one of the four industriesThe vertical playbook, plus verified BigOrange proof only12

How a deck gets made

  1. Scaffold a brief for the recipe; every slot the recipe needs is listed with a TODO.
  2. Fill the slots from the source material. Proof slots accept only verified BigOrange facts.
  3. Build. The validator runs automatically and fails on any leftover placeholder, missing logo or missing banner.
  4. Render every slide to an image and look at it. Fix overflow by shortening copy, not by shrinking type.
  5. File the deck with its brief beside it. Sending stays with a person.

Shipped with this package: four industry pitch decks (one per vertical), a kickoff example, and the Thursday review rebuilt through the engine as proof that the recipe reproduces it.

Vertical playbooks

Four industries, sourced and dated

One skill per industry BigOrange serves: custom home builders (deepest), IT and MSPs, manufacturing, landscaping. Each carries the same five files so the deck engine and any writer can use them the same way.

01

Industry brief

Who the buyer is, their customer's journey, what is usually broken, objections, proof patterns, KPIs, seasonality, compliance cautions. Every fact has a URL and a date; the file expires 2026-12-01.

02

Deck narrative

A slide-by-slide pitch arc with slot names and copy guidance in BigOrange voice. Slides that need verified proof are marked and cannot be auto-filled.

03

Keyword seed

Query clusters by intent with a suggested owner page. Volumes are marked unverified until a real tool run.

04

Content angles

Fifteen to twenty hooks grouped by buyer job, each naming the proof it needs before it ships.

05

Audit checklist (home builders)

Fifty-two yes/no questions across website, proof, local presence, search foundation, follow-up and measurement.

06

Fail-closed rule

No skill invents a statistic, a case study or a homeowner quote. If the evidence does not exist yet, the skill says so and points at how to get it.

06
Brand voice

How everything sounds

The Brand Voice Guide (separate PDF) is the long form. The short form travels inside the brand system so every deck, document and blog is written the same way.

  1. Write to one busy person as "you". Lead with their worry, not with us.
  2. Plain verbs: find, fix, show, check, build, book, grow.
  3. Question, short answer, proof. Keep the rhythm uneven.
  4. One playful line per section at most, followed by something useful.
  5. Every number carries a source and a date. Estimates stay labeled as estimates.
  6. No guarantees, no promised rankings, no invented case studies.

07
Adoption

What BigOrange decides

To fold this into the team's own operating skills, three decisions unlock the rest.

1

Confirm the per-surface brand rule: logo orange and Montserrat for decks and PDFs, the live theme for the website.

2

Issue a role-based, read-only WordPress Application Password (or connect the site in Abency) so the client radar can see real client names.

3

Name one factual reviewer and one release owner for anything the engine drafts.

4

Grant read access to Search Console, GA4 and HubSpot when monthly performance decks should carry live numbers.

5

Decide which industry pitch deck goes to a real prospect first, and who presents it. The engine can produce the next one in minutes; the proof on slide seven is the only part that cannot be automated.
