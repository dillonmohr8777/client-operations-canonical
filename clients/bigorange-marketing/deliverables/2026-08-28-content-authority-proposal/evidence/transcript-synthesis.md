## What Janice changed or clarified

### Audience and fit

Janice broadened the practical audience beyond a narrow custom-builder definition. BigOrange can serve custom builders, production builders, developers and master-planned communities, and potentially remodelers. For this pilot, she suggested keeping the central page focused on home builders rather than forcing remodelers into the same pillar.

Useful source moments:

- Custom versus production builder distinction: `main 01:30-02:40`
- Pipeline peaks and valleys for low-volume custom builders: `main 02:41-03:55`
- Developers, master-planned communities, patio homes, and second-home markets: `main 06:03-08:38`
- Remodelers as a future audience, while keeping this pillar builder-focused: `main 31:25-31:53`

Do not publish the tentative `$8M-$12M` fit threshold without Margee confirming the actual policy. Janice described it as an uncertain recollection at `main 02:41-03:12`.

### Trigger moments

The strongest reasons builders seek help are:

- succession or generational transition;
- leads drying up;
- an in-house marketer leaving or being stretched too thin;
- a website that still feels "recent" internally but is about six years old;
- word-of-mouth volume no longer supporting the desired pipeline;
- the need to smooth seasonal sales peaks and valleys.

Source: `main 03:35-06:02`.

### Trust and proof

Janice's clearest trust hierarchy is visual and relational:

- word of mouth introduces the builder;
- the website and social presence show an active, credible business;
- strong project photography earns attention;
- video testimonials and visible people/process reduce risk;
- personality fit matters because a custom-home relationship can last roughly two years;
- parade-of-homes and Homearama participation can provide local proof.

Source: `main 12:13-15:16` and `main 29:53-30:14`.

Photography guidance is specific: professionally captured kitchens and detailed vignettes, finished and furnished spaces where possible, twilight exteriors when landscaping is incomplete, and useful behind-the-scenes process footage. Source: `main 19:50-22:11`.

### Positioning

Janice identified three interchangeable claims: meticulous craftsmanship, great design, and walking the customer through the process. Her method for finding the real difference is a month of discovery, weekly conversations, customer interviews, ideal-customer analysis, and careful listening for an operating distinction such as team support versus direct owner access.

Source: `main 15:22-18:07`.

This resolves the pillar's SME placeholder. The final point should be paraphrased as: a real difference is not a slogan; it is a support, access, decision, or delivery pattern customers can actually experience and verify.

### Referrals and reach

Referrals remain useful, but become insufficient when leads dry up, when a builder wants relocating buyers who lack a local referral network, or when the company wants to shift its service mix. Source: `main 18:09-19:31`.

### Website and content priorities

The first screen must prove the quality of the work visually. Janice was skeptical that visitors read much copy before reacting to the photography. The page still needs clear text, but the opening should not bury the best project proof.

Missing or under-explained answers include:

- pricing or a responsible investment range;
- what site work happens before construction;
- who handles permits;
- whether the buyer needs a separate architect;
- how design-build differs from architect-led work;
- whether the buyer needs to bring land or whether the builder helps find a lot.

Source: `main 19:33-25:28`.

### Objections

The page should answer these in plain language:

1. "I can do the marketing myself."
2. "Referrals and word of mouth are enough."
3. "My work should speak for itself."
4. "What will I get for the money?"

Janice's strongest analogy: builders do not pound every nail themselves; marketing is another specialized part of the business that can be contracted to experts. Source: `main 25:31-28:50`.

### Search language and AI boundary

Natural market language includes `move-up buyers`, `custom homes`, `executive homes`, `estates`, and `luxury`. Source: `main 29:21-29:52`.

Janice did not provide a usable answer to the AI-answer-engine question and called it subjective and changeable. The final AI-search section therefore stays grounded in current official platform guidance, not a fabricated Janice position. Source: `main 30:14-31:25`.

### Lead quality and handoff

Janice described a marketing-qualified lead as a person who has raised a hand. She also noted that a poor investment fit may reveal a positioning problem. The conversation did not define geography, project type, decision-maker, lot status, timing, or working-style scoring in enough detail for a final CRM definition.

Marketing can create demand and bring in leads; sales still has to follow up and do the sales work. Source: `main 31:41-32:29`.

### First 90 days

Janice's healthy early state is a launched website and, if paid social or Google Ads are fast-tracked, the possibility of early leads. She was explicit that 90 days is not much time and that six months is a more realistic period to get the program rolling.

Source: `overflow 00:00-00:40`.

The proposal must not promise an early lead count. The first 90 days should be evaluated as implementation and signal validation; months four through six are the first momentum window.

### Permissioned builder example

Janice described a builder preparing for Homearama after handling marketing internally. The event produced a large volume of visitors, many of whom were not qualified. BigOrange refreshed the website before the show and created an email nurture series so people researching the builder afterward found a more credible destination and a follow-up path.

Source: `overflow 00:40-02:03`.  
Permission: `P` at `overflow 02:03-02:10`.  
Publication boundary: paraphrase only; omit the approximately 30,000-person figure unless Homearama or client evidence verifies the timeframe and BigOrange approves its use.
