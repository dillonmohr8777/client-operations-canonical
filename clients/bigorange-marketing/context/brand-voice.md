## The voice in one sentence

BigOrange sounds like a sharp Cincinnati marketing friend: seasoned enough to explain the hard stuff, warm enough to lower the pressure, and cheeky enough to call a pretty but useless website a cutie website.

## Website voice

- Put the customer and the problem first. BigOrange is the guide, not the hero.
- Use ordinary physical language: dragging rocks uphill, an already-full plate, getting found, bringing in leads.
- Explain the next step in plain English and make it feel low pressure.
- Pair warmth with proof: awards, reviews, timelines, results, or a defined plan.
- Prefer questions and short answers over long agency declarations.
- Use the signature citrus language lightly: squeeze the day, fresh, refreshing, juicy.

## Social voice

- Open with an irritation, opinion, joke, or current moment.
- Allow slang, fragments, parenthetical asides, and a little weirdness.
- Turn technical news into a direct consequence for the reader.
- Name the people behind the work and credit them enthusiastically.
- Use a short setup, a punchline or correction, then the useful truth.
- Keep the CTA literal: see the site, join the session, read the post, check the result.

## Writing rules

1. Write to one busy person as `you`.
2. Lead with what they are worried about or trying to get done.
3. Use plain verbs: find, fix, show, check, build, book, grow.
4. Put a human consequence next to every technical capability.
5. Follow every playful line with something useful or specific.
6. Keep the rhythm uneven: question, answer, proof; long, short, punchline.
7. Use one playful phrase per section at most.
8. State the source, date, number, and gap when presenting proof.
9. Do not promise magic. Say what will be built, checked, measured, or improved.
10. Make calls to action feel like help, not a funnel ambush.

## Favored language

Use: clear, simple, real, refreshing, practical, seasoned, genuine, guide, blueprint, get found, get leads, grow, trust, off your plate, next right step, show our work.

Avoid: unlock, elevate, empower, ecosystem, synergy, transformative, future-ready, robust capabilities, operating layer, connective tissue, revenue signal, compounding growth loop.

## Voice QA

Before publishing, ask:

- Does the section start with the reader or with us?
- Could a real person say this across a desk without sounding ridiculous?
- Is the benefit concrete?
- Is there a source or example behind the claim?
- Did we use a systems word where a real noun and verb would be clearer?
- Is the joke helping the point, or just trying too hard?
