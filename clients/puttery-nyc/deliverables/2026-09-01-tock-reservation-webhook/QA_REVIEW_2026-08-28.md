# Puttery NYC Tock Receiver Independent QA

**Review date:** August 28, 2026  
**Scope:** Local reservation ingestion scaffold, supporting specifications, acceptance cases, and exact-account monitor  
**Final judgment:** Local scaffold GO. Production webhook and conversion delivery NO-GO until the documented external and operating gates pass.

## Verified resolved

- The receiver is explicitly separated from the future normalized conversion ledger.
- Tock `COMPLETE`, `DEFERRED`, `ERROR`, and unknown refund states are separated; only completed refund value affects `completedRefundCents`.
- Placeholder business IDs, placeholder or short authorization values, invalid body sizes, and invalid ports are rejected.
- Same-version conflicts create a dedicated privacy-safe conflict record without replacing accepted state.
- SQLite state survives a process restart and still suppresses duplicate delivery.
- Tock uint64 reservation, business, and version identifiers remain exact decimal strings and are compared with `BigInt`.
- Canonical payload hashing prevents whitespace or object-key ordering from creating false conflicts.
- Non-NYC business-group events receive a successful filtered acknowledgment and retain no source identifiers.
- Guest PII, raw click values, arbitrary custom-field names, processor identifiers, and confirmation codes are excluded from stored safe summaries.

## Verification evidence

- Thirteen of thirteen Node tests passed.
- All receiver source files passed syntax execution through the test suite.
- All four JSON configuration, schema, and acceptance artifacts parsed successfully.
- The exact Tock Reservation model was checked for cent fields, `KeyValue`, uint64 IDs, payments, refunds, refund statuses, `partyState`, `sequenceId`, `confirmationCode`, and the Business object.

## Residual local limitations

- The SQLite health probe proves database readability but does not proactively simulate disk-full or read-only write failure.
- Conflict records persist safely, but the operational resolve and source-refetch procedure remains part of the production runbook.

These limitations do not block continued local integration work. They do block representing the endpoint, conversion ledger, or ad-platform delivery as production-ready.
