Sep 14 to Sep 20, 2026

One finding that is worth more than a week of optimisation, and a recommendation to act on it quickly.

Read your report

The bidding has been learning from junk form fills, and that needs stopping before anything else is worth doing.

Worth stopping now.

Two findings that are really one. A conversion goal on this account was pointing at the wrong action in the connected customer record system and was corrected on September 14. Separately, and more seriously, the automated bidding has been treating form submissions that are not real enquiries as successes since at least September 9. Because the bidding optimises towards whatever it is told counts, feeding it junk makes it better at finding more junk. A dated read showed $365.95 of spend in that stretch attached to submissions that do not qualify, and a direct review of one day found the contacts recorded were solicitations rather than prospective customers. The fix for the signal is written and has not been applied.

This is a work and delivery update. Current platform metrics are not established for this report, so they are not estimated.

The spend figure below is a dated read rather than a live pull for this exact week. It is labelled accordingly and has not been rounded up or extrapolated.

What moved forward

Specific actions, deliverables and decisions, with their evidence status.

Sep 14Verified work / decision

A conversion goal pointing at the wrong action was corrected

The account's conversion goal was pointing at the wrong action in the connected customer record system. It was repointed at the correct one.

It means the account had been optimising towards the wrong definition of success, which is the same class of problem as the one below and compounds it.

Correction applied and confirmed, Sep 14
Sep 16Verified work / decision

Automated bidding found to be training on unqualified form fills

Submissions that do not represent real enquiries have been counted as successful conversions since at least September 9, which is the signal the automated bidding uses to decide who to show ads to.

This does not merely waste the spend it touches. It teaches the system to go and find more of the same, so the problem compounds week on week until the signal is cleaned.

Account read, Sep 16

A dated read of the account recorded $365.95 of spend across the affected stretch, attached to submissions that do not qualify as enquiries. This figure comes from that dated read and not from a live pull covering September 14 to 20.

It puts a number on the cost of leaving the signal uncleaned, which is what makes the case for acting this week rather than next.
