Executive assessment

Nexla has generated meaningful search demand over more than four years, but the account has paid for scale without a reliable enough signal for qualified pipeline. The lifetime pattern is clear: spend is concentrated in a small set of campaigns, brand demand is materially stronger than nonbrand efficiency, and conversion data needs to be repaired before bidding or budget expansion can be trusted.

What the lifetime account is telling us

Spend is concentrated

The five largest campaigns account for $74,921.09, or 64.9% of lifetime spend: Data Integration ($34,570.80), Competitor Fivetran ($14,566.77), Data Products ($8,923.45), Demand Gen ($8,720.26), and Brand Exact ($8,139.81). This makes disciplined campaign separation more valuable than adding more campaign volume.

Brand is the clearest signal

Brand Exact has 2,418 lifetime clicks at a 20.30% CTR and $8,139.81 in spend. Protect it, keep it separate, and use it as a controlled baseline rather than allowing brand performance to mask nonbrand weakness.

Nonbrand efficiency is uneven

Data Integration spent $34,570.80 for 35 reported conversions at $987.74 per conversion and a 1.42% CTR. Competitor Fivetran spent $14,566.77 for 3.5 reported conversions at $4,161.93 per conversion and a 0.73% CTR. Data Products spent $8,923.45 for 4 reported conversions at $2,230.86 per conversion. These themes need query-level pruning and message-to-landing-page alignment before expansion.

Measurement is the largest risk

The account reports 133.28 lifetime conversions, a 0.31% conversion rate, and $866.07 reported cost per conversion, while the live account also reports an inactive conversion tag. Conversion reporting is pending validation; these figures should not be treated as qualified pipeline until the demo event, thank-you event, attribution window, and CRM outcome are reconciled.

Current spend is below the opportunity

In the latest seven-day benchmark, the account spent $206 and received 40 clicks versus approximately $520 and 144 clicks for comparable businesses. The answer is not to spend indiscriminately; it is to place the available $1,000 monthly ceiling behind the few themes that prove intent.

Creative and policy debt is visible

Google currently flags poor ad strength on G_US_S_NB_AI-Data-Integration, a missing lead form asset on G_US_S_NB_MCP-Agentic, and a policy-limited ad within G_US_S_NB_Data-Products. Recent diagnostics also show disapproved ads in Data Integration and Competitor Fivetran. These are fixable blockers to efficient learning.

The core recommendation

Keep Brand Exact intact, repair conversion tracking, and add one tightly governed nonbrand Search campaign centered on Agent Data Layer, MCP, enterprise data integration, and data products.

Next steps

  1. Repair and validate the demo conversion from ad click through the thank-you event.
  2. Review recent search terms and block irrelevant research, employment, education, and free-tool intent.
  3. Resolve the policy-limited ad and replace weak responsive-search-ad assets.
  4. Confirm the landing page and message match for each high-intent theme.
  5. Build the new campaign with Search Partners and Display Network off.
  6. Keep the campaign paused while Nexla reviews budget, targeting, copy, and tracking.
  7. Launch only after written approval, then review search terms and lead quality weekly.
  8. Import qualified CRM outcomes so future bidding learns from real pipeline, not form fills alone.

What success looks like

The first 30 days should establish trustworthy conversion measurement, reduce avoidable search-term waste, improve nonbrand CTR, and identify which themes create qualified sales conversations. The lifetime account gives us the baseline; the new campaign should prove incremental intent instead of simply adding more clicks.

Launch gate: campaign remains unpublished until tracking, landing page, budget, copy, and written approval are confirmed.
