## The thesis that reconciles ambition with fifteen hours a week

**Every offer ships with its agent.** Delivery hours per client is an acceptance
metric of the offer itself, measured monthly and printed on the scorecard. An
AI division whose product is Dillon's hours is not an AI division; it is a
consultancy with a chatbot. The September 4 pack's own arithmetic makes the
point: a ten-hour delivery week minus three clients' recurring allocation leaves
5.8 setup hours. That number only moves if the agents do the delivery.

## Economics, under the pack's own assumptions

`economics_v2.py` runs the September 4 pack's `economics.py` engine with the
reconsidered offers. $75 loaded labour, 10% sales allowance, $500 fixed,
full collection. Not revenue. Not profit.

| Scenario | Monthly revenue | Hours | Contribution |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| One client on each recurring flagship | $3,500 | 16 | $1,125 |
| Same, at double hours | $3,500 | 32 | −$75 |
| Two clients on each | $7,000 | 32 | $2,750 |
| Plus one Spec build in the month | +$3,500 once | +24 | +$1,250 once |

Double hours takes the recurring cohort below zero. That is the whole argument
for hours-per-client as an acceptance metric. (My first hand-computed draft of
these rows was wrong by $100; the engine's assertion caught it. The page renders
the engine's output, never typed numbers.)
