# AI in Slack in 5 Minutes

Audience: Momentum 360 account managers
Format: Short Loom-style screen recording
Target length: 5 minutes
Presenter: Dillon Mohr

## Recording setup

- Open the Momentum Digital Agency workspace in Slack.
- Keep real client messages and direct messages out of frame.
- Open a neutral channel or blank Slackbot conversation.
- Turn on cursor highlighting and captions.
- Record at 1080p in a 16:9 frame.

## 0:00 to 0:25 — The outcome

### On screen

Point to `Chat with Slackbot AI` in Slack's top bar.

### Narration

> We already have AI inside Slack. You do not need another website or a new workflow to get started. Click Chat with Slackbot AI in the top bar, or use Control, Shift, O on Windows. This opens your private work assistant. It can use the Slack channels and documents you already have permission to see, and it cannot give you access to anything outside those permissions.

## 0:25 to 1:10 — Find what needs attention

### Prompt card

> Which direct messages or mentions need my attention first today? Give me the top five with links and next actions.

### Narration

> This is the fastest way to start the day. Ask for a short ranked list, links, and a next action. If the first answer is too broad, follow up with something simple such as, “Only include client work,” or, “Turn this into a checklist.” You do not need to rewrite the full prompt every time. Keep the conversation focused and use short follow-ups.

## 1:10 to 2:00 — Catch up on a channel

### Prompt card

> Summarize the decisions and action items in #ai-tech-news from this week. Include owners and open questions.

### Narration

> When a channel moves quickly, name the channel, give a time window, and ask for a specific output. Decisions, action items, owners, and open questions is a useful default. You can also paste a link to one Slack thread and ask for only the context in that thread. Always open the linked messages before you rely on the summary.

## 2:00 to 2:50 — Draft a client update

### Prompt card

> Draft a short client update from this thread. Separate completed work, blockers, and next step. Do not send anything.

### Narration

> AI is best used as a first-draft partner. Give it the thread, tell it the structure you want, and explicitly say not to send anything. Review the names, dates, numbers, commitments, and tone before the message leaves Slack. The account manager is still responsible for the final client communication.

## 2:50 to 3:35 — Prepare a focused meeting

### Prompt card

> Create a 20-minute agenda for my meeting with [name] using this channel and the linked notes.

### Narration

> Give the meeting length, the person, and the sources. Then refine the result with short follow-ups such as, “Make it shorter,” “Put the decision first,” or, “Turn this into a checklist.” This is especially useful when you have ten minutes before a call and need the key context without rereading a week of Slack.

## 3:35 to 4:15 — Make it reusable for the team

### On screen

Show a neutral example of forwarding or copying a reviewed response into a channel.

### Narration

> Slackbot is personal, but the work can still be collaborative. When a prompt works well, share the exact prompt and the reviewed result in the relevant channel. That gives the team a reusable starting point. Remember that each person's answers follow their own Slack access, so two people may get different context if their channel permissions are different.

## 4:15 to 4:45 — Guardrails

### On-screen checklist

- No passwords, API keys, MFA codes, or payment details
- Verify names, numbers, dates, and commitments
- Review before sending or publishing
- Human approval before spend or account changes

### Narration

> Never paste passwords, API keys, MFA codes, payment details, or sensitive client data into a prompt. Verify the facts that matter. AI can draft, summarize, and organize, but it should not send, publish, spend, or change an account without the responsible human's approval.

## 4:45 to 5:00 — Close

### End card

> Start small: one real prompt, one reviewed result, one saved template.

### Narration

> The three habits are simple: give context, ask for a format, and review before action. Start with one real prompt today, review the result, and save the version that works for the team.

## Prompt library

### Daily priorities

> Which direct messages or mentions need my attention first today? Give me the top five with links and next actions.

### Channel summary

> Summarize the decisions and action items in #channel-name from this week. Include owners and open questions.

### Client update

> Draft a short client update from this thread. Separate completed work, blockers, and next step. Do not send anything.

### Meeting agenda

> Create a 20-minute agenda for my meeting with [name] using this channel and the linked notes.

### Quality check

> Review this draft for unsupported claims, missing decisions, unclear owners, and dates that need verification. Do not rewrite it yet.
