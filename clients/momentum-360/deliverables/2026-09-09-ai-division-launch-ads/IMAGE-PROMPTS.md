# Momentum AI — image generation prompt pack

For GPT Image 2.5 (or any image model). The direction is one specific tension:

> **analog paper craft × futuristic machine.**
> Not "futuristic" as chrome and neon. Futuristic as *a machine someone built by
> hand and taped into a notebook.*

That tension is the whole idea, and it's what makes this not look like every
other AI brand image. It also happens to be literally what the division sells —
"Built, Not Prompted."

Grounded in the existing house look (`google-aistudio-batch/design/scrapbook.jpg`):
bone paper, torn kraft, washi tape, brass brads, a vintage engraving, **exactly
one blue element**, flat-lay, top-left key light.

---

## The style block — paste this into EVERY prompt

```
STYLE: Tactile analog collage photographed flat-lay from directly overhead.
Bone-white paper ground (#F2EFE9). Torn kraft paper with visible deckled fibre
edges, translucent washi tape at the corners, a small brass brad, a steel
paperclip. Real paper grain and fibre visible. Lighting is a single soft key
from the top-left casting a tight contact shadow plus a wide soft ambient
shadow — physical depth from layered shadow, never from glow.

COLOR: neutral paper tones — bone, kraft tan, warm grey — with exactly ONE
saturated accent in deep marine blue (#155E86), and at most one lighter blue
highlight (#3897CC). No other saturated colour anywhere.

NEGATIVE / NEVER: no purple or violet, no indigo, no magenta. No neon, no glow,
no bloom, no lens flare, no light leaks. No glassmorphism, no frosted panels.
No floating gradient orbs or blurred colour blobs. No chrome, no iridescence,
no holographic sheen. No 3D-render plastic look. No text, no lettering, no
logos, no watermarks, no UI. Not centred and symmetrical — compose off-axis.
```

**Why each negative is there:** purple/indigo/magenta and neon glow are the two
loudest "AI made this" tells; glassmorphism and floating orbs are the next two.
Chrome and holographic sheen are what "futuristic" collapses into if you don't
forbid it. No-text because generated lettering is almost always subtly wrong and
you have real type to overlay.

## How to use these

1. **Generate wide, then cut hard.** Run every prompt 4–6 times. The style block
   holds the family together, so you can mix winners from different prompts.
2. **Compose off-centre deliberately.** Most of these ask for weighted, off-axis
   arrangements with empty space — that empty space is where the Archivo Black
   headline goes. A centred image is unusable as a backplate.
3. **Keep the one-blue rule.** The instant a second saturated colour appears,
   the set stops reading as a system. Reject those.
4. **Check the output against the anti-slop list** before you keep anything: any
   purple, any glow, any glass, any floating orb — bin it, regenerate.
5. **Generate at the largest size offered**, then downsample. These become
   backplates behind 148px type; softness shows.

## What to hand back for the films

For compositing into HyperFrames I want, per accepted image:
- the largest available render, PNG
- ideally a version with the object on a **plain flat ground** so it can be
  masked out if needed

Then the "machine" becomes a real photographed object in the films instead of
the abstract torus knot — which is what you actually asked for, and it kills the
blue-on-blue legibility problem in one move, since a paper machine is kraft and
bone with only a blue accent rather than a solid blue mass sitting behind blue
type.
