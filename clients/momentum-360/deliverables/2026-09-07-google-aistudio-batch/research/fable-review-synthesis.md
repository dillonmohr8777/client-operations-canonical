# Fable creative review — synthesis across all five ebooks

Run 2026-09-07, five independent `model: fable` reader passes, one per book, via the
`fable-advisor` skill (`dillon-os/.claude/skills/fable-advisor/`). Each reviewer read
as the specific owner the book is for, was told sourcing is already verified and out
of scope, and was asked only: does this read like a person, does it earn the reader's
time, does the ending earn the ask. Full transcripts are the session's own record;
this file is the actionable distillation.

**Headline: the sourcing pass and the reading pass measure different things, and this
book passed one and partly failed the other.** Every fact checks out. Four of five
books, read cold, lose real readers before the payoff — to the same handful of habits,
repeated across every book because one voice profile and one evidence bank fed all
five writers. Fix the habits once and the fix applies five times.

## The pattern, all five books

1. **Every book opens with a citation-key legend, not a hook.** "How to read the
   brackets" sits between the title and the first sentence addressed to the reader in
   all five drafts. It is the single largest, single easiest fix in the batch: move
   the legend to an endnote or the back matter. Every reviewer independently pointed
   at their own book's real opening line, already written, one paragraph later —
   usually Momentum's own worst number, stated plainly.
2. **Research notes bleed into reader copy.** Keyword-tool search volumes and SERP
   narration ("page one is a Reddit thread, then WebFX...") appear in four of five
   books, always early, always as the moment a skeptical reader would leave. An owner
   doesn't care what a keyword costs to bid on; that's the marketer's evidence of
   demand, not the reader's evidence of anything.
3. **Evidence gets dumped, not woven.** Every book stacks 3-7 anecdotes or repeats the
   same figures 2-3 times across chapters (the July call log, the 37-of-120 contrast
   figure, the $12-of-$25 spend). Once, each is a strong proof. Repeated, the reader
   notices the evidence bank being emptied onto the page rather than a person making
   an argument.
4. **Honesty gets announced instead of just happening.** "This is the chapter the book
   exists for. I'm going to tell it straight." / "Honesty clause:" / "I'm putting that
   in print on purpose." Four of five books do this. Every reviewer said the same
   thing: the admissions that follow are genuinely good; announcing them first is the
   tell of a performance rather than a person talking.
5. **Internal-plan language leaks into every About section and most CTAs.** Phrases
   lifted verbatim from `PLAN.md` — "finite delivery contracts," "exception queue,"
   "review budget," "cross-account write failures" — appear in reader-facing copy in
   all five books, concentrated in "About Momentum AI." This is the one place in every
   book where the person fully disappears.
6. **The CTA gets buried or undercut at the exact moment it matters.** FAQ + Monday
   checklist + About sit between the real emotional close and the actual ask in three
   books, draining it. A tagline ("Built, not prompted." / "Momentum built the
   machine...") lands after the ask in four books and reads as a different, colder
   speaker walking in for the last line. Book 4's CTA is split into two separate
   mentions with the offer catalogue wedged between.
7. **The best material in every single book, unanimously, is Momentum's own admitted
   failure, told plainly with a date and a number attached.** Not the ones dressed up
   (#4 above) — the ones just stated. This is the throughline across all five
   independent reviews: self-criticism is the asset; performing self-criticism is the
   liability.

## Book-specific findings worth acting on alone

- **Book 3 ("Built, Not Prompted") uses British spelling throughout** — colour, hon-
  our, labour, organised, anonymised — in a Philadelphia founder's first-person voice.
  Mechanical, not stylistic; a straightforward find-and-replace.
- **Book 3 uses "deliverable" three times** in the chapter about what the agency
  sells, despite the writer's own voice profile listing it as a word Dillon is
  measured never to use. A rules-pass miss the reading pass caught.
- **Book 4 ("The Small Business AI Operating System") carries the heaviest insider
  jargon load** — harness, token budget, estate, readback, decision-ready — words an
  owner would have to stop and ask about. Topic-specific risk: the most abstract book
  is the one most likely to slide into documentation.
- **Book 5 ("Names, Not Numbers"), chapter 6 — the central confession — is the single
  highest-stakes finding in the batch**, because this book is already gated
  unpublishable pending the Zap fix, and this chapter is why the book exists. Fable's
  verdict: **honest in the facts, evasive in five specific places in the framing**:
  - The subject goes passive at the exact moment responsibility should be named
    ("somebody finally asked a different question" — who?).
  - The chosen headline ("it wasn't late, it was impossible") reads from the client's
    side as an excuse — the tool did it — rather than owning the real failure, which
    is that nobody checked before making the first promise.
  - "Every one of those promises was made in good faith" answers a question the
    reader never asked and skips the one they did.
  - "One defect wearing thirteen costumes" is a clever line that quietly reframes
    thirteen broken promises as the origin story of a diagnosis — the closest the
    chapter comes to sounding proud of its own failure.
  - The chapter never says what the affected clients were actually told, in a book
    about promises made to clients.
  Fable's rewrite of the passage is in the full transcript and is a two-minute fix:
  same facts, first person restored, the omission named as the failure, the reveal
  kept for its own line instead of front-loaded in the "Short answer" box.

## What this does not change

Nothing here touches sourcing. No fact, figure, or claim flagged by any of the five
reviews — the sourcing/rules pass already covers that ground and this pass was
explicitly told not to re-litigate it. This is a reading-experience audit layered on
top of a verified-fact base, exactly as designed.

## Recommendation

The fixes are structural but narrow and identical across books: move each legend to
the back, cut the keyword-volume opener in each book to one sentence or delete it,
thin the repeated evidence to one strong instance per fact, delete the
"honesty-announcing" frames and keep the admissions, scrub `PLAN.md` phrasing from
every About section, and rebuild each ending so the ask is the last thing the reader
reads — no tagline, no FAQ, after it. Book 5 chapter 6 additionally needs the five
named framing fixes before it is fit to publish once it's unlocked.

This is real editing work, not a rubber stamp — probably 2-3 focused passes per book
rather than one. Not applied yet; this file is the brief for that pass, run
separately when authorized.
