## F. Why this works, and what I did not say

### Why this works (≤120 words)

The spot is two sentences long. "We built a machine. Now we build yours." opens
in handwriting on paper and closes in type on the mark; everything between is
proof-by-showing — a thing being folded, wired, laid out, checked, celebrated.
Every hero line is already on screen in the reference films, so the division
launches in a voice the brand has used, not a new one. Nothing needs the sound
on: the copy carries the argument and the VO (Option B) only repeats it. The one
promise on screen — "Built, not prompted." — is a statement about how the work
is made, which Momentum controls, not about what it will produce, which it
cannot guarantee.

### What I deliberately did not say

- **Named-lead match-back** ("we tell you which actual people your conversions were"). It is the sharpest claim available and the brief names it as the differentiator, but `PLAN.md` §Match-back says it must not be sold before the Zap fix lands — it is currently true for one account of four. Ready line for when it is fixed, Montserrat, on the map shot: `Names, not numbers.` Hold until then.
- **Any number.** No client counts, results multiples, hours, or turnaround claims. The plan itself corrects the one turnaround figure that was circulating.
- **Client names.** Omega, KJB, Onsite, Fresh Blends, Fagan all appear in the plan and none is authorised for use.
- **Guarantees.** Rankings, citations, leads, revenue — the offer table says "None," and "Built, not prompted." is chosen partly because it cannot be read as one.
- **"Never fails the design test."** True of the new design system's gate, not of shipped work (37 AA failures measured). Not a launch-spot claim.
- **Prices, tiers, "free audit."** All still awaiting Mac's sign-off per the plan.
- **Team or founder names.** No portraits, no bylines. The fireworks plate's composited portraits are not referenced in copy.
- **Hype vocabulary.** No "revolutionary," "cutting-edge," "unlock," "elevate," "seamless," "transform." Every display line is a plain declarative with a period.
- **"Momo" on the M-disc.** They are two characters; the paper city stays unlabelled.
- **"Always on."** Used only in Option C, only because it is verbatim in the Momo film. It is the softest capability claim in the estate; if it needs to go, C loses nothing.
