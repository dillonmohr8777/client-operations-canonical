Real geometry.

Seven subdivided planes are built in three.js — 72 × 44 segments for each video sheet, 40 × 76 for the long sheet — and displaced per vertex in an original GLSL shader. The curl toward camera, the travelling undulation and the surface normal that lights the paper are all computed on the GPU. A second pass distorts the whole rendered frame with a broad fluid ripple that is strongest at the sides and quiet through the centre, so copy on a settling panel stays readable.

Real-time capture depends on this machine keeping frame rate; it is a preview master, not a guaranteed frame-exact render. Frame-exact MP4 is prepared but not run here. The stage exposes renderFrameAt(t), which seeks every video and waits before drawing, so an offline harness can step 30 fps deterministically. Both are free and local; nothing was published, uploaded or sent to anyone. One film moves at a time. This browser will not decode three 1080p clips at once — two of three stall and never produce a picture — so the three video windows never overlap, exactly one clip holds a decoder, and every other video sheet shows a still captured from its own footage. At any instant one of your three films is moving and the other two are frozen on their featured frame. The offline render path has no such limit. Live video plays over the top during each clip's window and enhances the sheet; it does not decide whether there is anything to see. The live clip is rate-locked to its window rather than seeked, because seeking a playing 1080p element stalls it.
