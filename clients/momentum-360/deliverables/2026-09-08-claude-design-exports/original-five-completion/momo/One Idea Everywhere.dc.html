The paper becomes it.

Each of the four artefacts begins as a copy of the idea card, at the idea card's own position on the desk. It peels up, tumbles a full turn, and its texture is swapped at the halfway point of that tumble — so you watch one sheet of paper become a website, then a post, then an email, then an ad. Nothing cross-fades and nothing pops in as a finished card. Each landing then holds still for most of a second so the artefact can actually be read before the next one arrives.

Mock content,
labelled as such.

Every artefact is drawn from the Momentum palette and type and carries its own label: WEBSITE · ILLUSTRATIVE, SOCIAL POST · ILLUSTRATIVE, EMAIL · ILLUSTRATIVE, PAID AD · ILLUSTRATIVE. The email adds NOT SENT and the ad adds NO CLAIMS. There is no engagement figure, no conversion number, no client name and no result anywhere in the film. Momo's card carries a frame baked from the supplied Momo Reveal master, with the clip itself playing over it between 2.05s and 5.90s — chosen over the Watches the Money frame because Momo reads at hero scale there rather than small against a wall.

Implementation and export receipt

Deterministic: every card transform, camera position, look target and post value is sample(track, t), and the texture swap is a pure comparison against a fixed time. await stage.renderFrameAt(t) seeks the decoder, drains the serial queue and draws. stage.timingContract reports 20.00s, 30fps, 601 frames. One live decoder, by design. Only Momo's card carries moving video, and only inside its window; outside it the clip is detached and the card holds the frame baked from that same master. Attaching a source is not the same as having a picture, so the baked still stays up until the decoder delivers. The 9:16 master is recomposed, not cropped: its own authored track set puts the four artefacts in a tight block beneath Momo, redraws the thread as a vertical form, and opens the camera from 36° to 46°. Sound is off until you switch it on — synthesized sine partials, filtered-noise paper landings and a few marker tones, all generated in the page. No purchased track, no generated music, nothing licensed. Reduced motion holds the settled end frame at 17.90s and does not autoplay. Record master is a real-time capture at on-screen size, not a frame-exact 1920×1080 render.
