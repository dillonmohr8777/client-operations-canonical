A desk, not a slideshow.

Nine subdivided planes lie on a paper desk in three.js, rotated flat in world space so the camera can move around them. Each card is displaced per vertex in an original GLSL shader — a lifted leading edge plus a slow cross-sheet flex — with the surface normal rebuilt from finite differences, so paper catches the light as it drops and flattens. Cards arrive, land, hold still to be read, then gather. The post pass stays quiet on purpose: this film's motion belongs to the paper and the camera.

Your Momo.
Your masters.

The card Momo arrives on carries a frame baked out of the supplied Momo Reveal master, and the clip itself plays over that surface between 2.05s and 6.90s — the film's one moving decoder. The four task cards use the approved ceramic service icons on midnight plates at 14px radius. No character was regenerated, no face redrawn, and the wordmark on the end card is the exact master on a white plate.

Implementation and export receipt

Every card transform, camera position, look target and post value is sample(track, t) — a pure function of the film clock. Scrubbing to the same t produces the same frame. await stage.renderFrameAt(t) seeks the decoder, drains the serial queue and then draws, which is the hook an offline 30fps render would drive. One live decoder, by design. Only Momo's card carries moving video, and only inside its window; outside it the clip is detached and the card holds the frame baked from that same master. Attaching a source is not the same as having a picture, so the baked still stays up until the decoder actually delivers. Sound is off until you switch it on: three detuned sine partials through a 300Hz lowpass, plus soft filtered-noise paper landings and five marker tones, all synthesized in the page. No purchased track, no generated music, nothing licensed. Record master captures a composited surface — the WebGL frame with the same text beats drawn over it from beats(t), the single source the on-screen overlays also read — so the file cannot drift from the preview. It is a real-time capture at on-screen size, not a guaranteed 1920×1080 frame-exact render. The brief card is labelled SAMPLE BRIEF · ILLUSTRATIVE and the campaign sheet FOR HUMAN REVIEW · PROPOSED CONCEPT. No client, result, metric or deployment claim appears anywhere in the film.
