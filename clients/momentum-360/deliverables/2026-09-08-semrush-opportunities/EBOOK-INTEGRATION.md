## Chapter-level copy changes

These are insertion or replacement suggestions. They keep the books’ question-led structure and use one clear search intent per page. The Semrush facts below are labeled **Verified**; all proposed additions without a cited row are **Editorial hypothesis**.

### 1. Show Up When They Ask AI

- **Chapter 2 — “What is AEO, and how is it different from SEO and GEO?”** Add a short distinction after the definition: “A search report can show where a page was observed. It cannot promise that an AI answer will cite it. Treat the observation as a starting point for a page review, not as proof of recommendation.” This preserves the book’s existing caution.
- **Chapter 6 — “How do I get my business to show up in ChatGPT and Google’s AI answers?”** Add the local-service-page bridge: “Momentum’s current read already has the homepage at position one for `philadelphia digital marketing agency` and `philadelphia digital marketing`. The next job is to make the service facts that earned those observations easy to verify, then test the page against fixed questions.” **Verified rows, editorial action.**
- **Chapter 7 — “How do I measure AI visibility when there is no ranking?”** Add: “A broad filter is a sorting device, not a topic count. Keep the exact query, database, device, position type, URL, and observation date beside every row.” This turns the 352-row filter caveat into a reusable reader method without presenting it as new demand.
- **Chapter 8 — “What does AEO cost, and what should a retainer include?”** Replace any generic closing with: “Start with a fixed question set, the pages that answer it, and one dated observation sheet. The next step is a search visibility snapshot, not a promise of inclusion.” CTA: **Search visibility snapshot**.

### 2. From Missed Call to Booked Job

- **Chapter 2 — “What is an AI receptionist for a small business, and do I need one?”** Add the exact boundary from blog draft 05: “An AI receptionist is a routing and response workflow. Test hours, routing, unknown questions, escalation, and failure logging before calling it ready. A website chatbot and a phone answering system are different jobs.” **Editorial hypothesis; no Semrush metric in today’s report.**
- **Chapter 3 — “What should the first text say, and how fast should it go out?”** Add a small worksheet: “The first reply should identify the business, acknowledge the missed call, state the next human step, and offer one clear reply path. Log the time, number, message, owner, outcome, and opt-out state before adding more automation.” CTA: **Workflow audit**.
- **Chapter 6 — “How do I know it works before it touches a real customer?”** Add a replacement sentence for broad performance language: “Pass a fixed scenario set, inspect the exception log, and require a human takeover. A successful test proves the workflow handled those scenarios; it does not prove a future response rate.” This avoids inventing a result.
- **Film mapping:** pair the chapter opener with **Meet Your Next Team** and a transcripted clip of the lead-response role. Use **Momo: Ideas Into Motion** only for the transition to the automation consultation; do not imply that the film itself is a live receptionist.
