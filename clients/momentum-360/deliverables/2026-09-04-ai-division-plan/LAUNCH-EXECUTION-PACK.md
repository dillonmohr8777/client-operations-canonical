## 1. Lead Operations Pilot: proposed scope

**Buyer outcome:** a named employee can account for each accepted lead from one agreed intake source through one destination, including duplicates and exceptions. Proposed commercial terms: $3,500 setup and $1,500/month after accepted handoff. Setup budget 24 delivery hours; recurring budget six hours/month, including two support hours and one 30-minute review. Additional workflows or sources require a separate scope.

**Included:** documented field mapping; exact account/source/destination route; required-field validation; stable idempotency; duplicate and conflicting-replay handling; internal reply draft; durable exception queue; destination readback; simple operator report; rollback instructions; five supervised operator cases; one agreed improvement per recurring month. Use existing CRM and authentication rather than provisioning a replacement platform by default.

**Customer dependencies:** decision-maker and operator named; source and destination access approved; sample records with lawful use; authoritative mapping and data-retention policy; baseline lead volume and manual effort; reply claims/tone approved; testing window; recovery contact. Start the delivery clock only when the agreed dependencies are available. Log blocked dates, owner and resumed date; customer delay pauses the schedule without disguising vendor defects as access delays.

**Excluded:** autonomous outbound, lead-generation guarantees, extra CRM migrations, historical data cleanup, multi-location routing, payment processing, sensitive clinical/legal records, unlimited revisions, 24/7 support and unapproved third-party charges. Customer-facing replies remain drafts unless separately authorized.

**Acceptance:** agree 30 named cases before implementation. Required categories are normal valid records; malformed/missing fields; wrong account/source/destination; duplicate/reordered/changed-payload replay; concurrency; rate limits; timeout before/after commit; restart recovery; readback disagreement; exception visibility; operator acknowledgement and resolution; source unavailable; unauthorized access. All required cases must have an executed receipt. No failed critical case, missing case, wrong-client result or unresolved data-loss/routing defect can pass. Five supervised operator cases and written customer acceptance are separate from automated tests.

**Rollback:** stop the specific trigger; preserve input IDs and receipts; disable writes before changing mappings; restore last accepted configuration; reconcile already committed destination records by ID; replay only confirmed missing records after verification. Do not delete evidence or blindly replay ambiguous timeouts. Release only after a bounded dry run and destination readback.

**Support terms for the proposal:** Monday–Friday, 9am–5pm Eastern, excluding agreed holidays; acknowledge a blocking incident within one business day, with a workaround/status update before promising a fix date. This is a proposed acknowledgement commitment, not a resolution or uptime SLA. Critical routing defects immediately pause automation. Name primary/backup coverage before signing. Ordinary enhancement requests consume the agreed allowance; defects against acceptance require correction and are not relabeled enhancements.

## 2. AI Visibility Program: proposed scope and measurement

Proposed $1,500 setup and $1,500/month; one domain/location, 20 buyer questions, two specifically named engines, two useful page improvements/month, source-linked observation report and one consolidated revision. Ten setup hours, six recurring hours including review. Engines and access methods are selected during kickoff; no unsupported automated scraping or account setup is assumed.

Freeze the question set by intent: ten service/problem questions, five comparison/selection questions and five location/trust questions. Record exact query, engine/product/version if visible, run date/time, location/language, signed-in/out state, citations, brand mention, answer context, source URLs and screenshot/text evidence. Repeat the same questions on both engines at baseline and at the agreed monthly checkpoint. Mark errors/unavailable answers separately from non-mentions. If the interface is stochastic, use three repeat observations per question at each checkpoint and report the observed range; never cherry-pick the best answer.

Keep three measures separate: observed mention/citation frequency, verified referral traffic, and validated lead/customer outcomes. The denominator is successful observed answers, with attempted/error counts alongside. Baseline/change comparisons use matching questions and settings; changed engines or prompts create a new series. Do not infer causality from a before/after increase or promise rankings, mentions, leads or revenue.

Page acceptance: factual claim sources, useful answer to a buyer question, correct brand/location, working links, crawlability and canonical checks where accessible, mobile/editorial QA and approved publication target. Use existing analytics and source evidence; unavailable analytics remain pending. Exclude paid placements, broad PR/link procurement and unlimited content. Customer approves source truth, CMS access and final factual claims.

## 4. Twenty-minute discovery and qualification

| Minutes | Questions and evidence |
| --- | --- |
| 0–3 | What process is costing time or losing attention? Who owns it and who approves a change? Ask for one recent concrete example. |
| 3–7 | Walk one item from intake to completion. Which systems, fields, handoffs and exceptions exist? What happens after a failure? |
| 7–11 | How often does this occur? Obtain actual count/sample period and approximate human effort; mark estimates as estimates. Which business outcome matters? |
| 11–15 | Who grants access, validates records and uses the result? What must never happen? What data or contractual restrictions apply? |
| 15–18 | Select one finite pilot. Explain exclusions, test/acceptance approach, proposed price and support boundary. Ask what would make it a bad purchase. |
| 18–20 | Confirm next step, owner, missing evidence and date. No readiness or willingness-to-pay claim until the buyer actually states it. |

Score relationship approval, repeated problem, evidence availability, access feasibility and adoption owner 0–2 each. A zero in relationship/access/adoption prevents a build commitment. Eight to ten supports scoped proposal preparation; five to seven means paid discovery or evidence collection; below five means defer. This is an internal prioritization rule, not a validated predictor of sales.

Disqualify or defer: wants guaranteed AI ranking; has no authorized system access; requires sensitive-data handling outside capabilities; cannot name an operator; wants three CRMs and unlimited changes for the one-workflow fee; asks to misrepresent a prospect site as the official live business site. A low lead count alone is not disqualification if the process is valuable and measurable.

Missing collections stay unknown, not assumed paid. Report contribution only with the stated collection/cost basis; do not call it net profit.

## 8. Thirty-day pilot scorecard

Record client/offer, signed date, deposit collected, access-ready date, baseline, acceptance criteria/version, planned/actual setup hours, recurring estimate, actual tools, support/rework, test coverage, critical defects, operator cases, written acceptance and next review. Each metric needs a source and observation date. Keep scheduled, built, accepted, invoiced and collected stages distinct.

**Continue:** all mandatory acceptance checks and five operator cases pass, no unresolved critical defect, customer uses the workflow, and measured cost fits the agreed price/capacity. **Reprice or rescope:** quality passes but hours/support exceed the modeled allowance or scope repeats beyond the contract. **Pause/stop:** routing/data-loss risk, missing authorization, persistent failed acceptance, no adoption owner, or no defensible useful outcome. Commercial targets such as three paid pilots are goals, not criteria that override safety or evidence.

## 9. Worker-to-reviewer handoff

Reuse the existing [structural fixture](../../../../tests/fixtures/valid-worker-handoff.json) and [authoritative validator](../../../../scripts/Test-MarketingHandoff.ps1) in client-operations. The fixture contains synthetic, unrelated client and work-item IDs: replace these with the exact current queue record and version before preparing a real handoff. Do not copy the fixture as a Momentum completion receipt or invent a parallel queue. Paths in these links are relative to this document; the command below uses the absolute project root.

The JSON envelope accepts exactly these 23 fields; extra top-level fields are rejected:

| Fields | Type and constraint |
| --- | --- |
| `schemaVersion` | Integer `1`. |
| `handoffId` | Path-safe string, 8–160 characters, letters/digits/underscore/hyphen; starts with a letter or digit. |
| `workItemId`, `expectedWorkItemVersion`, `clientId` | Existing `wi-...` ID; positive integer matching the current queue version; exact active registry client slug (or null only for non-client work). Must match the queue record. |
| `workerRole` | Nonempty safe string, at most 80 characters. |
| `status` | `completed`, `partially_completed`, `blocked`, or `failed`. |
| `startedAt`, `finishedAt` | Date-time strings; finish cannot precede start. Use explicit UTC offsets. |
| `summary` | Safe string, 1–4,000 characters. Describe the result and next action accurately. |
| `artifacts`, `evidence`, `verification` | Arrays of strings, at most 10 entries each; each entry is nonempty and at most 1,000 characters. Artifact/evidence entries must be supported safe locators. Artifact paths must resolve inside the exact client folder; completed status requires nonempty evidence and verification arrays. |
| `assumptions`, `risks` | Arrays of safe strings, at most 5 entries each, at most 1,000 characters per entry. |
| `privacy` | Exactly `redacted`. |
| `containsSecrets`, `containsDirectIdentifiers`, `containsRawCommunications`, `canonicalWriteAttempted`, `externalActionAttempted` | Each must be boolean `false`. A worker that attempted a prohibited action cannot disguise it in a valid envelope. |
| `approvalGate` | `none`, `explicit`, or `human_authentication`; must agree with current queue approval state. |
| `proposedTransition` | One of `in_progress`, `verification`, `needs_approval`, `executed`, `observed`, `done`, `blocked`, `deferred`, `cancelled`; the validator also checks whether that transition is legal for the worker result and current queue status. |

Maximum encoded file size: 20 KB. Put workflow/node IDs, source hashes and timestamps, acceptance-case mapping, exact commands/results and extended limitations in a redacted client-scoped artifact referenced by `artifacts`/`evidence`; do not add unsupported top-level keys. An explicit pending approval allows only the appropriate gated transition; human authentication stays blocked or deferred. Do not alter queue approval state to make validation pass.
