# GT Clinic website and keyword audit

**Status:** Draft, internal, not sent or published  
**Observed:** 12 September 2026  
**Site:** https://thegtclinic.com/

## Summary

GT Clinic already presents a credible physician led aesthetic, regenerative, and functional medicine practice in Marlton, with clear consultation calls to action and several substantial service pages. Current search results confirm public pages for FAQ, contact, three nearby locations, and at least seven services. The strongest present coverage is aesthetic concern and treatment intent: hyperpigmentation, scar treatment, microneedling, laser vein removal, hair restoration, and the two GT Restore intimate wellness pages all have directly surfaced indexed pages.

The major gap is architecture, not a need to invent more services. The homepage currently carries a wide functional medicine promise, including sleep, inflammation and autoimmune concerns, digestive and gut health, and metabolic health and weight optimization. In the current indexed sample, no dedicated public page was verified for those decision paths. That is a thin existing coverage finding, not proof that a page is absent, because the site would not return a crawlable sitemap or HTML during this audit. The first two monthly pages should therefore make one approved functional medicine priority and one approved aesthetic decision path substantially clearer, rather than add generic blog volume.

The public retrieval result is itself important. Direct requests to the homepage, robots.txt, sitemap.xml, sitemap_index.xml, wp-sitemap.xml, and WordPress API endpoints all returned HTTP 202 with a SiteGround powCaptcha screen. Fourteen discovered page URLs were then attempted in a bounded crawl and likewise yielded the challenge instead of page HTML. The in app browser reached the same cookie based challenge. This does not prove Google or Bing are blocked and must not be described as an indexing defect. It does mean current titles, headings, canonicals, meta descriptions, internal links, schema, duplicates, page count, and performance cannot be revalidated from this network until access is supplied or the protection is adjusted for an approved audit route.

Search engines are currently surfacing the content despite that barrier. The homepage result was crawled six days before this audit. The FAQ and contact pages were also recently available in the public index. That supports work on clearer page ownership and safer proof, while reserving all technical claims that require source HTML for an access enabled rereview.

## Coverage and evidence limits

The page inventory contains 14 current public search discoveries. Direct crawl result: **0 of 14 yielded usable page HTML; 14 of 14 returned SiteGround HTTP 202 challenge content.** The sitemap and robots inventory also failed the same way. Search result titles and snippets are recorded as index evidence, not as a substitute for source inspection.

The earlier 21 August six template audit is useful only as dated context: canonical and meta description were present in that sample, one JSON LD block appeared on each sampled template, homepage H1 count was zero, blog H1 count was two, and the homepage mobile Lighthouse run recorded LCP 16.4 seconds and performance 29. Those facts are **unverified today**, not current findings.

## Top 10 findings

1. **P0, crawl observability is blocked from this audit route.** The challenge prevented all direct technical inspection. Verify robots, sitemap, WAF policy, real bot access, and indexed URL count in Search Console and host logs before changing technical controls.
2. **P0, consultation path is a general contact form.** Search-indexed contact content shows a text consent checkbox. Confirm the form completion event, referral source capture, service interest field, and confirmation behavior before Ads or conversion reporting.
3. **P1, functional medicine intent is broad on the homepage but not proven page specific.** Map the clinic approved first priority to one physician reviewed page. Candidates should be selected by the clinic, not assumed from symptoms or demand estimates.
4. **P1, current service coverage is unevenly verifiable.** Seven specific service pages are visible in current search, while Botox or Xeomin, fillers, laser hair removal, biostimulators, and GT Restore Lift are named in visible service menus but their target pages were not independently retrieved. Confirm one canonical destination for each.
5. **P1, Voorhees has a poor current result title.** The indexed title is `Regenerative-Medicine-Voorhees-NJ`, a slug style label that wastes the search snippet. Replace after source inspection with a natural local title tied to the actual service relationship.
6. **P1, location templates carry repeated service lists and similar FAQs.** Cherry Hill, Moorestown, and Voorhees each use distance, physician credential, service list, and consultation FAQ patterns. Retain real travel information, then add locally distinct, supported context and links to the relevant service page. Do not scale city pages from interchangeable copy.
7. **P1, comparative and proximity language needs evidence review.** Current snippets use claims such as nearest or closest. Validate each against a current, defensible source or soften it. This is content governance, not medical advice.
8. **P1, physician proof should resolve once, then support service pages.** Homepage and FAQ snippets identify Dr. Farooqui and physician performed care. Confirm the expert page has current credentials, a reviewer date, and links from each high intent service page.
9. **P2, FAQ content exists but page level answer ownership is unclear.** The general FAQ contains extensive answers. Move or link service specific questions to the page that should win that query, then keep the FAQ as a navigational hub and validate FAQPage markup only where eligible.
10. **P2, no current rank, volume, competitive, or conversion gap can be claimed.** Search Console, GA4, Business Profile, booking, and Ads data were outside this audit. The keyword map is an intent and coverage plan, not a demand forecast.
