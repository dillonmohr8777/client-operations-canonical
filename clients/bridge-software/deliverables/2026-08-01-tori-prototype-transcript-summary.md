## Executive summary

Tori's reaction to the prototype is strongly positive. She likes the Bridge metaphor, signal language, purple and dark palette, and core positioning. In her follow-up email, she called the prototype beautiful and said it exceeded her expectations.

She sees the current prototype as a strong foundation, not the final product. Her main request is to make it feel more like a specific, usable cannabis-industry network: clearer navigation and visual hierarchy, stronger profile and discovery tools, flexible content publishing, verified business access, and a nationwide feed that users can filter without becoming overwhelmed.

## Tori's product feedback

### Brand and visual direction

- Keep the purple and dark visual system. She finds it easy on the eyes and aligned with the brand.
- Keep the Bridge and signal concepts, including the core line "One industry, one network, one bridge."
- Add more imagery, animation, hover behavior, and visual movement. The current homepage feels too flat and text-heavy in places.
- Reduce the dominance of the large hero typography so the supporting features are easier to notice and understand.
- Add photos to the horizontal content strip.
- Rework the line about connecting without turning people into metrics. Tori wants Bridge to be a useful marketing and measurement system, so the current wording conflicts with the product goal.
- She sees merchandise and phrases such as "Get on the Bridge" and "Just get on the Bridge" as part of the launch brand.

### Navigation and information architecture

- Make the main sections more explicit and less jumbled: profile or home, explore, create, and community news or news feed.
- Rename "Exchange" to "Explore."
- Consider renaming "Business" to "My Profile" or a home-base concept.
- Simplify the search experience.
- Rename "Community" to "News Feed" or "Community News" so its purpose is immediately clear.
- Tori prefers a Pinterest or digital-newspaper style staggered feed. The other participant prefers a simpler Instagram-style vertical feed. This remains a design choice to prototype and test.

### Content creation and audiences

- Let users upload existing PNG, PDF, and image assets, not just build content inside Bridge.
- Add "Promotion" as a distinct post type alongside product introductions, vendor announcements, and events.
- Let one post reach multiple audiences when appropriate, including adults 21 and older, verified retailers, and industry professionals.
- Protect sensitive business content, especially wholesale pricing and menu information, based on the viewer's verified role.
- Tori likes the current Create experience and wants it developed further rather than replaced.

### Profiles, verification, and privacy

- Business profiles should show the correct sales representative and accounting contact.
- Send a recurring verification email and use a required first-login prompt to confirm or update profile contacts.
- Separate public or member access from verified business access. An EIN is the proposed basis for business verification.
- Tori initially preferred preventing vendors from following other vendors to protect competitive pricing. During the discussion, she recognized that public-market precedents and workarounds may make a total restriction impractical. The exact vendor-visibility rule remains unresolved.
- Expand the verified industry directory beyond brands and dispensaries to include banks, transporters, testing laboratories, manufacturers, construction firms, HVAC providers, electricians, and other cannabis-industry services. Credential and reference checks would be needed.

### Discovery and consumer utility

- Build Bridge as a nationwide legal-state network, not only a local or geofenced product.
- Add filters for state or location, product type, brands, strains, and other useful categories.
- Add favorites so users can follow the parts of the network they care about without making the default feed too narrow.
- Make brands and products easy to search, including categories such as pre-rolls, edibles, and concentrates.
- Consumer-facing dispensary profiles should support a live menu or order link, directions through Apple Maps, and links to relevant social or marketplace profiles such as Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, and Leafly.

### Monetization and launch strategy

- Tori wants Bridge to replace several fragmented marketing systems with one connected network.
- The discussion converged around an early brand price near $349 to $350 per month, potentially paired with six months free, a card on file, an annual commitment, and grandfathered pricing. This was exploration, not a finalized offer.
- Major brands are important because their participation creates value for the rest of the network.
- She believes launch must build both sides at once: business and brand recruitment plus event-based and street-level consumer acquisition.
- She wants strategic guidance on which side should receive the earliest emphasis without creating a chicken-and-egg failure.
- The team discussed a roughly fourteen-week summer build window, but Tori does not want to rush the launch. She would rather launch well, with event support and merchandise, than launch prematurely.

### Longer-term HR concept

- Tori raised a future idea for around-the-clock HR support for small cannabis businesses through a partner company.
- The discussion identified material privacy, legal, handbook, and role-access concerns. This is not ready for the initial product and needs defined parameters and legal review before inclusion.

## Suggestions Tori explicitly supported from the other participant

Tori responded positively to direct asset uploads, multi-audience posts, profile-verification prompts, simpler discovery, category filters, the consumer dispensary utility features, deliberate launch timing, and an accessible early brand price. These can be treated as jointly validated directions even though the other participant first proposed some of them.

## Unresolved decisions

1. Pinterest or newspaper-style feed versus a simpler Instagram-style feed.
2. How much vendor-to-vendor visibility is practical without exposing sensitive pricing.
3. The exact public, consumer, professional, retailer, and EIN-verified permission model.
4. Final launch pricing and the conditions attached to a free introductory period.
5. Which side of the marketplace receives the greatest early acquisition emphasis.
