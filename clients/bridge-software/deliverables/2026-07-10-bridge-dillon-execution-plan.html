02 · Immediate start

Phase 1: Discovery, Requirements, and Architecture

This is the confirmed starting point. The goal is to leave discovery with enough clarity that the team can build the right MVP instead of guessing at features, roles, or technical boundaries.

Before the prototype walkthrough

  • Review the contract and the project goal: a working MVP product.
  • Review the Phase 1 tracker and prepare a lightweight capture template.
  • Prepare questions about users, permissions, verification, listings, messaging, and admin controls.
  • Set up a note-taking and decision log workflow so product decisions do not disappear into meeting notes.
  • Coordinate with Miraj on what frontend decisions depend on backend architecture.

During the walkthrough and onboarding

  • Capture the product concept in the client’s language, including what Bridge must and must not do.
  • Record each user type, their objective, the actions they need, and the information they must provide.
  • Separate must-have MVP features from later ideas, nice-to-haves, and unresolved questions.
  • Identify compliance-sensitive points such as age gating, license status, jurisdiction, and business verification.
  • Confirm the happy path and the failure path for each important workflow.

Phase 1 outputs I will produce

Product map

A concise description of the MVP, users, core objects, and the main journey from entry to value.

Role matrix

What each role can see, create, edit, approve, message, and administer.

Initial backlog

Prioritized feature list with acceptance criteria, dependencies, unknowns, and a recommended build order.

My Phase 1 handoff standard: someone who did not attend the walkthrough should be able to understand the user roles, core screens, MVP priorities, open questions, and frontend implications from my materials.

05 · How I will operate

Working rhythm, dependencies, and guardrails

My recurring operating rhythm

  • Before a meeting: review the latest decisions, prepare questions, and know what must be decided.
  • During a meeting: capture user needs, constraints, decisions, owners, and unresolved questions.
  • After a meeting: update the application map, backlog, role matrix, and decision log.
  • During build: work in small vertical slices, show progress early, and surface blockers quickly.
  • Before handoff: verify the primary paths, record known limitations, and make the next action obvious.

What I need from the team

  • From Tori: prototype access, product priorities, role definitions, compliance/legal direction, and timely decisions.
  • From Melissa: organized client questions, research findings, meeting coordination, and feedback routing.
  • From Miraj: architecture decisions, data models, API expectations, environment access, and technical constraints.
  • From Mac: scope decisions, escalation support, priorities, and Phase 1 sign-off.

Boundaries that protect the project

  • I will not make legal or regulatory determinations; I will surface questions and translate confirmed guidance into product behavior.
  • I will not place sensitive client credentials, business verification data, or personal information into AI prompts or unapproved systems.
  • I will not silently expand the MVP. New features become backlog items until scope and ownership are clear.
  • I will not treat a polished screen as complete until the workflow, permission model, error states, and acceptance criteria are also covered.
  • I will keep frontend decisions synchronized with Miraj’s backend and architecture work.
  • I will make assumptions visible and label them for confirmation.
  • I will use the bridge/remote-browser workflow only for approved, task-specific access and document what was changed.
  • I will report blockers with evidence, impact, and a proposed next step.
Definition of done for my lane: the product can be explained in plain language, each role has a clear path, the frontend matches the agreed data and permission model, primary workflows are tested, and the team has a written backlog for what comes next.
