# What Should a Full-Service Landscape Contractor in Vacaville Handle?

**Short answer:** A full-service landscape contractor should do more than install plants. The contractor should evaluate how the yard functions, then coordinate grading, drainage, irrigation, planting, concrete or pavers, retaining walls, lighting, access, and cleanup so the finished space works as one system.

Vacaville homeowners often start with one visible goal: replace a lawn, add a patio, widen a walkway, clean up a side yard, or create a better backyard. But each choice affects the others. A patio changes drainage. A retaining wall changes planting depth. New beds need irrigation. A driveway extension changes the front-yard layout.

The value of a full-service contractor is coordination. Instead of asking several crews to solve separate pieces, the homeowner gets one plan for how the outdoor space should look, drain, and function.

## What is the difference between landscape maintenance and landscape construction?

Landscape maintenance keeps an existing yard in shape. It may include mowing, trimming, cleanup, fertilizing, and routine irrigation checks.

Landscape construction changes the property. It can include demolition, grading, drainage, irrigation installation, planting, concrete, pavers, retaining walls, lighting, fencing, steps, and outdoor-living features.

Homeowners planning a renovation should confirm which type of service a company provides. A crew that maintains lawns may not be equipped to build structural hardscape. A concrete-only crew may not plan planting, irrigation, and finish work around a new slab.

## What should happen before a landscape project is priced?

A useful consultation begins with how the property is used. The contractor should ask about parking, pets, children, entertaining, pool access, shade, maintenance preferences, water use, storage, and future plans.

The site review should also look for practical constraints:

- Where does water collect or flow?
- Is the yard flat, sloped, or terraced?
- Are there roots, utilities, irrigation lines, or access limits?
- Which areas get full sun or afternoon heat?
- How will materials and equipment reach the work area?
- What existing concrete, pavers, walls, or fencing will remain?
- Does the project affect a property line, public right of way, or area that may require permit review?

A price without a clear scope can be misleading. The proposal should identify what will be removed, what will be installed, the materials or allowances, major assumptions, and who is responsible for approvals if required.

## Should hardscape be designed before plants?

Usually, the functional layout should come first. Patios, walkways, driveways, steps, retaining walls, and outdoor rooms define where people move and how the yard is used. Planting then softens those areas, provides shade and texture, and connects the construction to the home.

That does not mean plants are an afterthought. Mature plant size, root behavior, sun exposure, and irrigation zones should influence the hardscape plan. The point is to design the pieces together.

For example, a new backyard may include:

1. A concrete or paver patio sized for dining and circulation.
2. A walkway from the side gate.
3. A small retaining wall to create a level planting bed.
4. Drip irrigation grouped by plant water needs.
5. Trees or structures placed for useful shade.
6. Low-voltage lighting at steps, paths, and gathering areas.
7. Gravel, mulch, or groundcover that finishes exposed soil.

When these elements are planned in sequence, the yard feels intentional instead of pieced together.

## How should a contractor plan for Vacaville heat and water use?

Vacaville’s long warm season makes shade, irrigation, and material selection important. A full-service plan should consider how surfaces feel in direct sun, where afternoon shade is needed, how plants will be watered while they establish, and how much ongoing maintenance the homeowner wants.

Water-wise landscaping does not require a bare yard. It can combine climate-appropriate plants, smaller lawn areas where turf is useful, mulch, drip irrigation, trees, concrete or paver paths, and defined outdoor-living zones.

The contractor should group plants with similar water needs and keep irrigation from spraying onto walls, fences, or hardscape unnecessarily. Drainage and grading should direct water safely rather than sending it toward the house, a neighbor, or a low spot in the patio.

## What should be included in a landscape-construction proposal?

A clear proposal may include:

- Demolition and haul-away.
- Site preparation and grading.
- Drainage work included in the scope.
- Concrete or paver dimensions and finish.
- Retaining-wall material and general scope.
- Irrigation zones, controller work, or line changes.
- Plant sizes or allowances.
- Mulch, rock, soil, and bed preparation.
- Lighting fixtures or allowances.
- Cleanup and final walkthrough.
- Known exclusions and change-order process.
- Permit or engineering responsibility when applicable.
- Payment schedule and expected project sequence.

Ask how unexpected conditions will be handled. Roots, buried concrete, damaged irrigation, soft soil, and poor drainage are sometimes discovered only after demolition begins. A professional contractor should explain the process for documenting and approving changes.

## How do you compare landscape contractors in Vacaville?

Do not compare only the bottom-line number. Compare scope, accountability, and whether each proposal solves the same problem.

Before hiring, ask:

- Is the contractor properly licensed for the work being proposed?
- Who will supervise the project?
- Will the company use its own crew or subcontract major parts?
- Who coordinates concrete, irrigation, planting, and drainage?
- Can the contractor explain why the layout works?
- How will access, cleanup, and protection of existing property be handled?
- What is the communication process during construction?
- What follow-up is included after completion?

California homeowners can verify licensing and review home-improvement guidance through the [California Contractors State License Board](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/).

## Frequently asked questions

### What does a landscape contractor do?

A landscape contractor builds or renovates outdoor spaces. Depending on the company’s scope, that may include grading, drainage, irrigation, planting, concrete, pavers, retaining walls, lighting, fencing, and outdoor-living features.

### Is a landscaper the same as a landscape contractor?

Not always. “Landscaper” can describe maintenance or installation work. A landscape contractor typically handles construction and should be properly licensed for the work being offered.

### Should I hire separate concrete and landscaping companies?

You can, but the plans and schedules need close coordination. One qualified team can reduce gaps between concrete, drainage, irrigation, planting, walls, and finish work.

### What should I bring to a landscape consultation?

Bring photos or examples you like, a list of how the yard should be used, known drainage or irrigation concerns, maintenance preferences, and any future plans for pools, structures, parking, or utilities.
