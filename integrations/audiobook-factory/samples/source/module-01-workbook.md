# Character: The Code of Self-Leadership

## Module One: Why Competence Stops Being Enough

Every leader is promoted for competence. Almost none are removed for lacking it.
They are removed for character: a decision made under pressure, a promise quietly
dropped, a room where the truth was available and nobody said it.

That is the gap this program exists to close. Technical skill gets you the
position. Character determines whether anyone follows you once you have it.

Consider the arc of a typical career. In the 1st decade you are rewarded almost
entirely for output. In the 2nd, you are rewarded for judgment. By the 3rd,
roughly 85% of what determines your effectiveness has nothing to do with the
skill that got you promoted in the first place.

## The P.A.C.T. Framework

Character is not a personality trait you either have or lack. It is a practice,
and like any practice it has components you can name, observe, and improve.

The P.A.C.T. Framework names four:

1. Presence. You are actually in the room, undivided, before you are anything else.
2. Accountability. You own the outcome, including the part that was not your fault.
3. Courage. You say the true thing at the moment it costs you something.
4. Trust. You are the same person in every room, whether or not it is convenient.

See Figure 1 below for how the four components reinforce each other.

Presence without Accountability is charisma. Accountability without Courage is
compliance. Courage without Trust is volatility. The four are load-bearing
together and unstable apart.

## Presence: The Discipline of the Undivided Room

Presence is the least glamorous of the four and the one most leaders fail first.

Attention is the only resource you spend that you cannot recover, borrow, or
delegate. When a leader is half-present, the people in the room do not conclude
that the leader is busy. They conclude that they do not matter.

The practice is unforgiving and simple. Before a conversation, name the outcome
you owe the other person. During it, remove the thing that is competing for you.
After it, write down what you committed to. That is the whole discipline.

## What Module Two Covers

Module Two takes up Accountability, and specifically the distinction between
accountability and blame. Blame looks backward and searches for a person.
Accountability looks forward and searches for a system. Leaders who confuse
the two build teams that hide problems until the problems are too large to hide.
