Every claim carries its evidence tier.

Direct owned-channel, CRM-confirmed source, and assisted are separated and labelled rather than blended into one headline number. That is what makes the $162,000 defensible in a room with a CFO in it — the tier travels with the figure.

Nine qualified buyers had no logged follow-up at all, and seven of those were hot. Marketing had produced them and the business never touched them. Including the highest-scoring lead in the entire cohort — a buyer whose path from an AI assistant to a conversion was captured end to end, at a moment when almost nobody had that instrumented.

That finding is worth more than the revenue number. An attribution layer that only flatters the marketing team is a reporting tool. One that surfaces a 41% follow-up gap is an operating instrument — and it changed what the business did next.

Why this transfers

The same problem, one industry over.

This was HCM software sold to HR and finance buyers through a partner-heavy channel. The measurement problem in that market is structural, and it is the same one everywhere.

What the pipeline actually looks like in this space

Partner-sourced pipeline dwarfs everything else, which is exactly why owned-channel contribution gets dismissed in this industry — it looks small next to a reseller relationship. The website line only becomes an argument when someone can trace it deal by deal. That tracing is the work.

What I would do in the first ninety days

  • Establish the evidence tiers before the dashboard. Agree what counts as direct, assisted and correlated, in writing, with sales and finance. Every attribution argument that fails, fails here first.
  • Instrument the AI-assistant path deliberately. Buyers now arrive from ChatGPT, Perplexity and AI Overviews carrying real intent. Most CRMs file them as Direct and lose them. Recovering that traffic from Direct was one of the highest-value things this layer did.
  • Publish the follow-up coverage number alongside the lead number. A lead nobody calls is not a marketing win. Reporting both is what made this credible internally.
  • Write the content that owns the comparison queries. Vendor-selection and migration terms are where the buying decision happens, and they are winnable without paid media — 67% of this footprint came from blog content.

The short version. I built the marketing programme, then built the ledger that proved what it was worth — $162,000 closed-won and $2.19M in supported open pipeline — and the same ledger showed the business it was dropping qualified buyers on the floor. I would rather bring both halves of that than only the flattering one.
