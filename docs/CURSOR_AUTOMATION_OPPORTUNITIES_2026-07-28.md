# Cursor Automation Opportunities

Prepared July 28, 2026 from the current client-operations workflows.

## Operating model

Cursor is most valuable as the persistent repo worker: it can turn structured inputs into code, graphics, PDFs, dashboards, QA evidence, pull requests, and repeatable generators. The Marketing Chief remains the canonical client router and approval layer for live accounts, secrets, sending, publishing, spend, and consequential changes.

## Highest-return repetitive workflows

| Rank | Workflow | Cursor should produce | Human or Marketing Chief gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Monthly content factories | Calendar, finished copy, branded graphics, weekly client-review PDFs, master packet, manifest, and QA report | Legal or brand review, duplicate check, scheduling, and publication |
| 2 | Weekly performance reporting | Fresh client-specific dashboard build, PDF or deck, source ledger, metric validation, and change summary | Source-account verification, interpretation, and delivery |
| 3 | Inbox and Slack request conversion | Redacted request brief, exact client route, proposed deliverable, owner, dependencies, and draft response | Canonical queue write and any reply or external action |
| 4 | Landing-page production | Page code, responsive QA, accessibility check, tracking plan, screenshots, and deployment candidate | Correct client, domain, conversion destination, and publication |
| 5 | Content repurposing | Blog-to-social, newsletter, short-form video script, FAQ, GBP draft, and campaign variants | Factual review and channel approval |
| 6 | Paid-media monitoring | Read-only pacing, delivery, tracking, anomaly, and creative-fatigue briefs plus dashboard refreshes | Budget, bid, targeting, campaign, or account changes |
| 7 | HubSpot and customer-agent QA | Acceptance suites, knowledge-source audits, regression tests, handoff tests, and issue-ready evidence | Portal identity, activation, CRM mutations, and customer-facing release |
| 8 | CRM and attribution hygiene | Duplicate candidates, source gaps, lifecycle inconsistencies, form-to-CRM test results, and reconciliation reports | Record merges, deletes, lifecycle changes, and attribution-policy changes |
| 9 | SEO and local-audit production | Technical audit, competitor gap, content brief, page recommendations, screenshots, and client PDF | Claims, priorities, and production-site edits |
| 10 | Repo maintenance | Test runs, dependency and CI fixes, documentation, code review, release notes, and pull-request drafts | Merge and deployment according to the exact repo policy |

## The next three systems to build

1. **Portfolio reporting factory:** one schema per client and channel, one command to refresh every weekly dashboard and create its source ledger and review packet.
2. **Request-to-deliverable processor:** read-only Gmail and Slack intake becomes a redacted, exactly routed brief and then a local draft or artifact; it never sends on its own.
3. **Reusable content engine:** the BOK generator becomes a template with client brand tokens, series rules, disclaimers, source requirements, and PDF layouts so other clients can receive the same production depth without cross-client blending.

## Proven pattern from the BOK batch

The July 28 BOK build uses one structured `content.json` source to generate 32 square graphics, eight five-page weekly PDFs, one 41-page master PDF, complete copy, a calendar, contact sheets, hashes, and deterministic QA. Changing the content source and rerunning the generator reproduces the complete package.
